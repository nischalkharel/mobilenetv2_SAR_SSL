# Cross-dataset transfer and negative transfer of SSL pretraining in remote sensing (optical and SAR), scene-level pretraining evaluated on small-object / chip tasks

Compiled 2026-11-25. Scope: literature up to November 2026. All facts carry an inline source; anything not found is in Gaps with the queries tried. Numbers are quoted as reported by the source (or by a fetch of the source); where a fetch only saw an abstract, that is stated.

Our own claims being checked against the literature:
(a) DINO / LeJEPA SSL on 94k SARDet-100K scenes transfers to ATRNet-STAR vehicle chips WORSE than ImageNet init;
(b) cutting SSL views around label-free bright regions of the scenes fixes it;
(c) the same pattern repeats on FAIR-CSAR;
(d) SSL on a dataset's own scenes helps its chip classification only after enough steps.

---

## Key question 1: Did remote-sensing SSL / foundation models ever report NEGATIVE transfer (below ImageNet initialization), and in what settings?

### Takeaway
Yes, but it is scattered and almost always framed as "ImageNet remains competitive" rather than "negative transfer". The cleanest documented cases: (i) a Nov-2026 matched-pair study where two EO encoders (DINOv3-SAT, SatMAE) trail their generic twins on every scene dataset, by up to 22.5 linear-probe points, with losses concentrated on small man-made object classes and largest at 1-5 % labels; (ii) Corley et al. (CVPRW 2024) where ImageNet ResNet-50 beats SSL4EO-MoCo, SatMAE and Scale-MAE on UC Merced and TreeSatAI; (iii) Lahrichi et al. 2025, where ImageNet SSL beats same-recipe Sentinel-2 SSL in 13/36 linear-probe and 12/36 fine-tune comparisons; (iv) in SAR, SARATR-X's own ablation where SAR-only MAE on a ViT trails supervised ImageNet at every shot count, SSL4SAR (TGRS 2025) where SSL-from-scratch on Sentinel-1 gives 574-773 m error versus 342 m for plain ImageNet weights, and Inkawhich 2025 where the satellite-pretrained DINOv3-SAT scores below web DINOv3 on SAR target chips. No paper was found that reports the specific combination "SSL on large SAR scene archive -> below ImageNet on fine-grained vehicle chips".

### Cited findings

**Optical, scene classification / segmentation**

- Agrawal, Sathish, Nambiar, "Satellite Pretraining Is Not One Thing: A Matched-Pair Study of Generic and Earth-Observation Foundation Models for Scene Classification", arXiv 2611.13849, 10 Nov 2026, accepted GeoCV workshop @ WACV 2027. Five matched pairs (DINO/SatDINO, DINOv3/DINOv3-SAT, MAE/SatMAE, MAE/Scale-MAE, CLIP/RemoteCLIP), frozen encoders, kNN / linear / attentive probes, six low-label splits, PatternNet, RSICD, EuroSAT, UC Merced, RESISC45 (0.06-30 m GSD). "significantly hurts two (linear probe: DINOv3-SAT by up to 22.5 points, SatMAE by up to 8.8)"; "Pretraining on satellite imagery is thus not a reliable selection criterion." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13849)
- Same paper, 10 % label table (kNN -> LP, generic -> EO): DINOv3 ViT-L -> DINOv3-SAT on UC Merced 84.9 -> 43.2 kNN, 95.2 -> 72.7 LP; on RESISC45 87.0 -> 59.6 kNN, 92.1 -> 79.7 LP. MAE ViT-L -> SatMAE on UC Merced 47.5 -> 41.6 kNN, 87.2 -> 78.4 LP; on RESISC45 84.3 -> 77.8 LP. "SatMAE trails MAE ViT-L in linear probe on all five datasets" (drops 1.2 to 8.8). DINOv3-SAT trails "on every dataset and metric in all six splits". The two MAEs were pretrained on the same fMoW-RGB imagery yet Scale-MAE helps and SatMAE hurts: "we cannot separate the contributions of the data and of the recipe". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849)
- Same paper, small-object concentration: the EO effect is "concentrated on classes defined by small man-made objects"; DINOv3-SAT loses 34.7 / 34.4 / 33.0 LP points on basketball court / palace / tennis court; SatMAE loses 19.9 / 19.5 / 17.4 on palace / airport / storage tank; natural classes (forest, desert, lake) barely move. Label-scarce regime: "Gaps of either sign are largest when labels are scarcest"; at 1 % labels DINOv3-SAT trails DINOv3 by 46.1 LP points on UC Merced and 28.2 on RESISC45, versus 8.0 and 6.1 at 50 %. Offered (non-causal) explanation: "consistent with the different native resolutions of the pretraining corpora" (0.6 m Maxar for DINOv3-SAT); "our experiments do not establish this mechanism causally". Recommendation: "candidate checkpoints must be validated on target data". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849)
- Corley, Robinson, Dodhia, Lavista Ferres, Najafirad, "Revisiting pre-trained remote sensing model benchmarks: resizing and normalization matters", arXiv 2305.13456 (May 2023), published CVPR 2024 Workshops (PBVS). "ImageNet pre-training remains a competitive baseline for satellite imagery based transfer learning tasks". KNN(k=5) numbers: UC Merced 256 px: ImageNet ResNet-50 90.70 vs SSL4EO-MoCo 85.50, Scale-MAE 85.1, SatMAE 84.2, ConvMAE 81.7 (Scale-MAE "is outperformed by ImageNet pretraining in the UCM dataset"). RESISC45 256 px: ImageNet 77.48 vs SSL4EO-MoCo 73.24, SatMAE 77.1 (but Scale-MAE 85.0, ConvMAE 78.8 win). TreeSatAI RGB: ImageNet F1/mAP 40.37/40.58 vs MoCo 37.68/37.57. EuroSAT RGB: ImageNet 91.17 vs MoCo 95.76 (SSL wins). Resizing 64 -> 256 px gives ~10 points on EuroSAT for ImageNet ResNet-50; correct preprocessing gives +32.28 (So2Sat) and +11.16 (EuroSAT) OA. "one would assume that the SSL pretrained model clearly outperforms ImageNet pretraining" but "this is not the case". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13456); [CVPRW 2024 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/PBVS/papers/Corley_Revisiting_Pre-trained_Remote_Sensing_Model_Benchmarks_Resizing_and_Normalization_Matters_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)
- Lahrichi, Sheng, Xia, Bradbury, Malof, "Is Self-Supervised Pre-training on Satellite Imagery Better than ImageNet? A Systematic Study with Sentinel-2", arXiv 2502.10669, 15 Feb 2025 (preprint). Same recipe (SwAV ResNet-50 400 ep; MAE ViT-B 400 ep) on GeoNet (1,228,895 Sentinel-2 images, 224 px) vs ImageNet-1k; six downstream tasks at 64/256/1024 training samples. Counting strictly-higher scores, ImageNet wins 13 of 36 linear-probe and 12 of 36 fine-tune comparisons (e.g., FT EuroSAT n=64: MAE-IN 0.78 vs MAE-GN 0.74; FT DeepGlobe 0.5 m n=1024: SwAV-IN 0.68 vs SwAV-GN 0.65; LP LandCoverNet n=64: MAE-IN 0.33 vs MAE-GN 0.29). Two-stage IN -> GN beats GN-only on 5/6 tasks but "usually 1-2%". ImageNet has ~4x more unique pixels (473x405 vs 224x224 average image). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10669v1)
- Marsocci, Jia, Nascetti et al., PANGAEA benchmark, arXiv 2412.04204 (Dec 2024). 12 GFMs (CROMA, DOFA, GFM-Swin, Prithvi, RemoteCLIP, SatlasNet, Scale-MAE, SpectralGPT, SSL4EO-S12 MoCo/DINO/MAE/Data2Vec) vs supervised UNet and ViT-B/16. At 10 % labels CROMA has 6 top-2 placements vs UNet 2. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204v1)
- Romero, Lv, Karimzadeh, "How do Self-Supervised Remote Sensing Vision Models Transfer to Downstream Tasks?", arXiv 2606.13896, 11 Jun 2026 (preprint). SSL4EO MoCo/MAE/DINO, Prithvi v1, CROMA, TerraMind vs supervised ImageNet ViT-B/16 (13-channel first layer). "model rankings shuffle across the panel of diverse tasks". Frozen ImageNet is "generally not strongly competitive" but on PANGAEA Farms at 10 % labels ImageNet 32.27 beats MAE 23.60 and TerraMind 12.54; on Floods ImageNet 79.26 beats MAE 78.76; fine-tuned ImageNet on Sen1Floods11 at 10 % labels 84.36 vs GeoFM mean 83.22 ("ranks 2nd place"). Frozen aggregate score at 10 % / 100 % labels: web DINOv3 ViT-L 64.0 / 69.5; DINOv3-SAT (Maxar) 63.4 / 69.1; SkySense 65.8 / 69.2; TerraMind (Sentinel-scale) 55.1 / 61.9; Prithvi-EO 2.0 36.4 / 52.8; ResNet-50 57.7 / 63.7. **SAR**

Pretraining set 186,600 SAR target samples from 14 datasets (chips plus sliced detection scenes: "we slice bigger image sizes than 1000 pixels to increase the number of samples and extract small target slices"). ### Inferences
- The optical literature now contains three independent 2025-2026 studies (Lahrichi; Agrawal; Sharma) plus Corley 2024 and PANGAEA 2024 saying ImageNet/web pretraining is at least competitive and sometimes better, with the losses concentrating on small man-made objects and at 1-5 % labels. This is the same shape as our finding (a), but on optical scene classification, not on SAR chips.
- In SAR, every documented below-ImageNet case is either (i) a weak SSL recipe (I-JEPA, LoMaR, plain MAE on ViT) or (ii) a tiny pretraining set (SSL4SAR 9.5k images). Nobody reports a large (~100k) SAR scene archive with a strong recipe (DINO) transferring below ImageNet to vehicle chips. Our finding (a) therefore seems new in the SAR setting; the closest analogue is Inkawhich's DINOv3-SAT < DINOv3-Web on ATRNet-STAR/MSTAR chips (25.6 vs 30.8), which is optical-satellite pretraining, not SAR-scene pretraining.
- All SAR foundation models that work (SARATR-X, AFRL-DINOv2, XuanJian-DINOSAR, SARATR-X-v2) start from ImageNet / DINOv2 weights; SARATR-X's ablation and SSL4SAR both show from-scratch SAR SSL losing to ImageNet init. Our comparison should state explicitly whether the SSL runs start from scratch or from ImageNet, because the field treats "ImageNet then SAR SSL" as the default.

### Gaps
- No paper found with the exact pairing "SSL on SARDet-100K / FAIR-CSAR scenes -> worse than ImageNet on ATRNet-STAR chips". Queries tried: "pretrain on SAR scenes fine-tune target chips domain gap self-supervised 2026"; "SAR self-supervised pretraining target recognition MSTAR transfer ImageNet comparison 2025"; "SAR ship classification OpenSARShip FUSAR-Ship self-supervised pretraining worse than ImageNet initialization"; "SAR foundation model survey 2025 2026 negative transfer chips scenes MSTAR ImageNet pretrained better".
- No paper uses the phrase "negative transfer" for RS SSL; the framing is always "ImageNet remains competitive" or "not a reliable selection criterion".
- SARATR-X-v2 (arXiv 2607.23238v4, Sep 2026; 320K images incl. FAIR-CSAR, M4-SAR, ATRNet-STAR train) has no ImageNet-only baseline under its protocol, so it cannot say whether SAR SSL beats ImageNet init on ATRNet-STAR; ATRNet-STAR SOC-50 5-shot LP: iTPN-L 98.4 vs AFRL-DINOv2 94.9. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)
- The 2026 SCIENCE CHINA SAR foundation-model survey was not fetched (PDF); its snippet claims "progressive self-supervised pre-training can yield more robust SAR visual representations than supervised transfer" without numbers. — [survey PDF](http://scis.scichina.com/en/2026/180301.pdf)

## Key question 2: Which papers choose SSL crops around objects, targets or saliency in remote-sensing scenes? Proposer, gain, random-placement control?

### Takeaway
Object-aware view selection for SSL is well established in natural images (Object-Aware Cropping, CAST, SoCo, DETReg) with gains of several mAP, but in remote sensing it is rare and, where it exists, uses either ground-truth boxes from detection datasets (OCMIM, MutDet, ISSL, MSP-O) or attention / scattering priors for MASKING rather than for CROPPING (RingMo PI-Mask, SAMBA, OCMIM's attention-guided mask). No remote-sensing paper was found that (i) proposes crops from a label-free brightness / CFAR / saliency detector on SAR scenes and (ii) reports a random-placement control; the only crop-position control found (MA3E) shows a 0.15 mAP / 0.19 accuracy gain over random positions.

### Cited findings

**General-domain precedents (natural images)**

- Mishra et al., "Object-Aware Cropping for Self-Supervised Learning", TMLR 2022 (arXiv 2112.00319): replace one or both random crops with crops from "an object proposal algorithm"; "achieves an improvement of 8.8% mAP over random scene-level cropping using MoCo-v2 based pre-training" on OpenImages; rationale "encourages the model to learn both object and scene level semantic representations". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00319)
- Selvaraju et al., CAST ("CASTing Your Model: Learning to Localize Improves Self-Supervised Representations", CVPR 2021): crops constrained so that each covers at least a set fraction of an unsupervised saliency map; Grad-CAM supervision toward salient regions. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04630)
- Wei et al., SoCo ("Aligning Pretraining for Detection via Object-Level Contrastive Learning", NeurIPS 2021): selective-search proposals treated as instances. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02637)
- Survey: "Self-Supervised Learning for Real-World Object Detection: a Survey" (arXiv 2410.07442) covers CAST, object-aware cropping, DETReg (selective search) vs UP-DETR (random crops). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07442v2)

**Remote sensing (optical)**

- Zhang et al., "Object-Centric Masked Image Modeling-Based Self-Supervised Pretraining for Remote Sensing Object Detection", IEEE JSTARS vol. 16, pp. 5013-5025, 2023. An "object-centric data generator" builds pretraining data around objects "to capture object-level context across scales and categories" and an "attention-guided mask generator" masks high-attention regions; motivation: "under a high random mask ratio, scene-level MIM struggles to capture small objects in complex remote sensing scenes"; evaluated on six RS detection benchmarks. Whether the generator uses ground-truth boxes or is label-free could not be verified (IEEE and repository pages blocked). — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10129022/); [UGR repository](https://digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/103727)
- Huang et al., MutDet ("Mutually Optimizing Pre-training for Remote Sensing Object Detection"), ECCV 2024 (arXiv 2407.09920): object embeddings from a pretrained backbone aligned with detector features; "When using 10% of the DIOR-R data, MutDet improves DetReg by 6.1% in AP50"; a search snippet of the paper says self-supervised pretraining (e.g., SwAV) "demonstrates inferior performance in remote sensing detection compared to supervised pre-training on ImageNet" (not verified from abstract page). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09920)
- "Instance-Semantic Self-Supervised Learning for Object Detection in Remote Sensing" (IEEE, 2025/26, doc 11415374): instance branch + object-semantic branch with contrastive learning, pretrained on RS detection datasets, DOTA / DIOR, largest gains with scarce labels (details from search snippet only). — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11415374/)
- "MSP-O: A Two-Stage Mask Pretraining Method With Object Focus for Remote Sensing Object Detection" (IEEE, 2025, doc 10906620) — title only, not fetched. — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10906620/footnotes)
- Li et al., MA3E ("Masked Angle-Aware Autoencoder for Remote Sensing Images", ECCV 2024, arXiv 2408.01946): the only crop-position control found in RS SSL: choosing the rotated-crop position with their scaling center-crop "results in an improvement of 0.19 in classification accuracy and 0.15 mAP in detection compared to randomly choosing positions"; the authors call the gain limited. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01946v1)
- RingMo (Sun et al., TGRS 2023) PI-Mask: keep some pixels inside each masked patch, "tailored to the complex backgrounds and small targets characteristic of RS data" (as described in RingMo-Aerial, arXiv 2409.13366, and the "Genealogy of Foundation Models in RS" review, arXiv 2504.17177). Masking, not cropping. — [RingMo-Aerial](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13366v2); [Genealogy review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.17177)
- CropSTS (Remote Sensing 2025, 17(14):2481) adopts an "Object-Aware DINO" framework for cropland; cropping details not confirmed. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/14/2481)

**Remote sensing (SAR)**

- "Scattering Characteristics-guided Self-supervised Learning for Target Classification in SAR Images" (ACM, 2024): scattering priors integrated into contrastive SSL for aircraft chips; "1.48% increase in accuracy compared to the original self-supervised method" on a self-built dataset. Operates on chips, not on scene crops. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3647649.3647669)
- SAMBA (Wang et al., arXiv 2606.31668, Jun/Jul 2026): "three-level hierarchical Scattering-Guided Masked Autoencoder (SG-MAE) masking strategy guided by SAR physical priors"; "SG-MAE strategy further boosts the model's few-shot transfer capability"; masking, not cropping; mechanism and data not in abstract. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31668)
- SARATR-X (above): sampling is target-chip plus "automatic target slicing" of large detection scenes using the detection annotations; the authors flag "automatic target slicing" as an open issue. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365)
- SARATR-X-v2: only random-resized-crop (scale [0.2, 1.0]) to 224 px, "does not describe scene tiling or object-aware sampling". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)
- SAFE (Muzeau et al., arXiv 2407.00851, 2024): masked Siamese ViT with global/local crops, despeckled teacher view, sub-aperture augmentations; no CFAR-based crop selection; "applicable across multiple SAR acquisition modes and resolutions". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00851)
- FG-MAE (Wang et al., arXiv 2310.18653, 2023): Canny / HOG / SAR-SIFT features as MIM reconstruction targets for SAR; guidance in the target, not in the view sampling. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.18653)
- TRANSAR: Gaussian-blob targets "designed to segregate small, point-like objects" and a curriculum sampler that balances foreground/background per batch; chip selection is random; no saliency crop. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13310)
- CFAR in SAR deep learning is used as a detector or a feature source (2P-CFAR candidates + 14 features; CFAR-modified Faster R-CNN), and pseudo-labels appear in semi-supervised detection (multi-teacher D-S fusion; CSRFA cross-platform self-training), but no paper uses CFAR / bright-region detections to choose SSL views. — [2P-CFAR](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362988356_Ship_detection_based_on_a_superpixel-level_CFAR_detector_for_SAR_imagery); [D-S fusion](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202406.1318); [CSRFA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271625002795)

### Inferences
- The "views around label-free bright regions" idea (our finding b) has a clear natural-image lineage (object-aware cropping +8.8 mAP; CAST; SoCo) that we should cite as precedent, and a SAR physics lineage (CFAR, scattering priors) that has so far been used for masking or targets, not for view placement. Combining the two with a random-placement control appears to be unreported in SAR.
- The one RS crop-position control (MA3E, +0.15 mAP) suggests that in optical scenes crop placement matters little; our SARDet detection result (RegionDINO +1.5 mAP but random control equal, from the memory notes) is consistent with that, while the cross-dataset chip result (object-only +9.4 / +7.1 / +1.4) is the part without precedent.
- SARATR-X's authors list "automatic target slicing" of large scenes as an open problem; our bright-region proposer is a direct answer to that.

### Gaps

## Key question 3: Crop scale / GSD / native-vs-downsampled resolution for SSL in remote sensing

### Takeaway
Scale is treated as a first-class problem in optical RS SSL (Scale-MAE ICCV 2023, Cross-Scale MAE NeurIPS 2023, SatDINO 2025, X-STARS 2024), but the evidence is about (i) encoding GSD or (ii) robustness to test-time downsampling, not about whether to pretrain on native tiles versus downsampled whole scenes. Reported effects are small (SatDINO: +/- 0.5 points from view-scale ranges; GSD encoding +0.2-0.3) except for robustness to downsampling (Scale-MAE loses 2.6 points at 50 % GSD vs 5.0 for vanilla MAE). One study finds "no direct correlation between the GSD of pretraining and downstream tasks" and that matching input size between pretraining and evaluation matters most. Benchmarks consistently find that encoders pretrained on coarse (Sentinel-scale) imagery transfer worst to VHR tasks. In SAR, no paper studies native tiles vs downsampled scenes for SSL.

### Cited findings

- Reed et al., Scale-MAE, ICCV 2023 (arXiv 2212.14532): GSD-scaled positional encoding and Laplacian-pyramid decoder; "Models are generally unaware of absolute scale when learning over a set of data". kNN (k=20) averaged across GSDs: Scale-MAE vs SatMAE vs ConvMAE: RESISC 70.0 / 61.0 / 67.0, UC Merced 75.0 / 69.8 / 70.0, WHU-RS19 79.5 / 78.5 / 77.0, EuroSAT 86.7 / 84.4 / 88.8 (ConvMAE wins there); average gains 5.6 over SatMAE and 2.4 over ConvMAE. RESISC-45 ablation (ViT-B, 300 ep): vanilla MAE 77.8 at 100 % GSD -> 72.8 at 50 % (-5.0); Scale-MAE 80.7 -> 78.1 (-2.6). "Scale-MAE provides the largest performance gap at the 2.4m GSD" on UC Merced. No ImageNet-MAE comparison. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.14532)
- Tang et al., Cross-Scale MAE, NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv 2401.15855): scale augmentation plus cross-scale contrastive and generative consistency, no GSD needed; retains a lead "even when images were downscaled to 12.5% of their original size"; outperforms SatMAE and Scale-MAE in kNN "across various datasets and scale ratios" except one dataset; adding GSD positional encoding "reduced performance in one setting". — [NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3fadcbd0437f4717723ff3f6f7216800-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15855)
- Straka and Gruber, SatDINO, arXiv 2508.21402 (Aug 2025). fMoW-RGB (412,965 images). "In the context of remote sensing, cropping and resizing also alter the ground sample distance (GSD) of the image"; "satellite imagery often requires identifying features that occupy only a small portion of the image"; "The size of the local view can dramatically affect the class of the image." Scale ablation (Table 6b, kNN / top-1 on fMoW): local [5,25] global [25,100]: 68.89 / 71.22; local [5,50] global [50,100]: 69.18 / 71.11; local [25,50] global [50,100]: 66.25 / 67.89 (narrow local scale hurts ~3 points). Uniform scale selection (Table 7b): widening local range to [5,100] lowers fMoW kNN to 67.30 but raises RESISC45 82.21 -> 83.25 ("expanding the local view range, improved performance on downstream tasks"). GSD-encoding weight ablation moves results by <= 0.7 points. Summary table: uniform scale + GSD encoding = +0.31 kNN, +0.44 top-1 on fMoW, +0.21 RESISC45. SatDINO "falls behind MAE-based models at higher resolutions" (segmentation at 512 px). No ImageNet DINO baseline. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21402)
- Marsocci and Audebert, X-STARS ("Cross-sensor self-supervised training and alignment for remote sensing"), arXiv 2405.09922 (May 2024): "there appears to be no direct correlation between the GSD of pretraining and downstream tasks"; EuroSAT top-1 with (pretrain size, eval size): (224,224) 94.6; (224,64) 90.9; (100,224) 93.7; (100,64) 87.3; (300,224) 92.9; (300,64) 91.8; "The highest accuracy of 94.6% is achieved when both pretraining and linear evaluation input sizes are" 224; "looking for a trade-off between using the highest resolution and maintaining an appropriate patch size". ImageNet-MAE baseline vs X-STARS (kNN/linear): EuroSAT 90.7/89.9 vs 94.4/95.5; UC-Merced 75.1/65.7 vs 89.9/91.7; SpaceNet-8 mIoU 67.9 vs 67.6 (ImageNet slightly ahead). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09922v1)
- Corley et al. 2024: resizing from 64 to 256 px alone gives ~10 points on EuroSAT with an ImageNet ResNet-50; "Resizing images does not result in significantly changed downstream performance with the RCF methods". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13456)
- PANGAEA 2024: "GFMs pre-trained on lower-resolution data tend to perform worse on high-resolution datasets". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204v1)
- "When GeoFMs Meet VHR" 2026: Sentinel-scale encoders (TerraMind 55.1 / 61.9; Prithvi-EO 2.0 36.4 / 52.8) transfer worst to 0.1-1.5 m tasks vs web DINOv3 64.0 / 69.5; continued DINOv3 pretraining on 1,018,163 VHR 256 px tiles gives 65.2 / 70.0 (+1.2 at 10 % labels, +0.5 at 100 %); on NZ Cattle detection at 10 % labels 59.1 -> 67.0 mAP@50. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)
- Agrawal et al. 2026: small-object losses are "consistent with the different native resolutions of the pretraining corpora" (0.6 m Maxar for DINOv3-SAT) but "do not establish this mechanism causally". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849)
- UAV multispectral SSL paper (arXiv 2607.11366, 2026): notes the "commonly used scale resizing in SSL pretraining" of 0.2-1 and that Scale-MAE "requires known GSD values" (snippet only, text garbled). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11366)
- SAR: SARATR-X-v2 defines scale in fixed pixel receptive fields (3x3 to 35x35 supports); "has no ablation that varies input resolution"; random-resized-crop scale [0.2, 1.0]; images center-cropped and bilinearly upsampled when short side < 64 px. Multi-scale softmax target raises MSTAR 1-shot from 71.7 (pixel target) to 93.4 with iTPN. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)
- SAR: Natsume, Yun, Yeo, "Data-Efficient ConvNeXt Pretraining for High-Resolution Sentinel-1 GRD", arXiv 2610.21993 (Oct 2026): halves the stem stride for "4x more spatial tokens at identical parameter count"; "classification saturates with as little as 10-25% of the SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 corpus" while "dense prediction continues to improve significantly up to 25-50%"; no ImageNet comparison. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21993)

### Inferences
- The field has studied "encode the scale" and "be robust to test-time rescaling", not "which scale to pretrain at for a chip task". A direct window-size sweep on SAR scenes with a chip-classification endpoint (our ViewScale study) has no published counterpart that I could find.
- The strongest published analogue to "downsampled scenes hurt small-object transfer" is the repeated benchmark finding that coarse-GSD pretraining transfers worst to VHR / small-object tasks (PANGAEA, VHR-Bench, Agrawal). These are correlational across different models, not controlled within one recipe.
- X-STARS' finding that matching pretrain/eval input size matters more than GSD supports presenting our crop-scale choice in terms of object-pixels-per-token rather than metres.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found of native-resolution tiles vs downsampled whole scenes for SSL with an object-chip endpoint, optical or SAR. Queries tried: "ground sampling distance crop scale self-supervised pretraining remote sensing resolution"; "scale mismatch pretraining downstream GSD transfer remote sensing self-supervised high-resolution vs low-resolution pretraining study"; "Cross-Scale MAE SatMIP scale-aware masked autoencoder remote sensing multi-resolution downsampled native results".
- "SatMIP" returned nothing; it may be a misremembered name.
- Scale-MAE's per-scale-ratio kNN values (12.5 / 25 / 50 / 100 %) are only in a figure, not in text.

## Key question 4: Remote-sensing SSL backbones on fine-grained object chips at low label budgets (1-10 %): typical gains

### Takeaway
On SAR vehicle / ship / aircraft chips, SSL that starts from ImageNet and continues on ~100-320k SAR images gives very large few-shot gains over ImageNet-supervised init (e.g., MSTAR 10-shot fine-tune 70.3 vs 16.7; SAR-VSA 5-shot linear 76.5 vs 49.0-58.6; ATRNet-STAR average NN accuracy 79.6 vs 30-31 for off-the-shelf DINOv2/v3). Gains shrink or reverse when the SSL recipe is weak (I-JEPA, LoMaR, plain MAE on ViT) and vanish for data-rich regimes (BIDFC: ImageNet fine-tuning wins above 1:3 data). On optical chips/scenes the matched-pair study finds gaps of either sign are largest at 1-5 % labels (+15.4 for Scale-MAE vs MAE on RSICD at 1 %; -46.1 for DINOv3-SAT vs DINOv3 on UC Merced at 1 %).

### Cited findings

- SAR-JEPA (arXiv 2311.15153): MSTAR fine-tune 10/20/40-shot 70.3 / 82.1 / 91.6 vs ImageNet 16.7 / 32.0 / 40.7; linear 67.7 / 75.1 / 81.6 vs 45.0 / 56.3 / 66.8. FUSAR-Ship FT 81.3 / 83.3 / 85.8 vs 54.0 / 57.0 / 59.7. SAR-ACD FT 54.8 / 62.6 / 75.5 vs 22.6 / 26.8 / 42.7. "ImageNet weights cannot easily obtain effective features with small samples when fine-tuning." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15153)
- SARATR-X (TIP 2025): MSTAR SOC 10-way 1/2/5-shot 85.2 / 91.4 / 95.9 (BIDFC 80.7 / 85.3 / 90.3; DKTS-N 49.3 / 58.5 / 72.3); SAR-VSA 25-class linear 5/10/20-shot 76.5 / 80.8 / 85.1 vs supervised ImageNet HiViT 49.0 / 55.8 / 63.3, ViT 58.6 / 65.7 / 74.2, ConvNeXt-V2 52.5 / 61.7 / 70.5. Few-shot evaluated with linear probing "as HiViT overfits when many parameters are trained". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365)
- SARATR-X-v2 (arXiv 2607.23238v4, Sep 2026): MSTAR SOC 5-shot linear probe iTPN-B 96.3, iTPN-L 98.7 (+2.8 over SARATR-X 95.9); ATRNet-STAR SOC-50 5-shot iTPN-B 97.8, iTPN-L 98.4 (+3.5 over AFRL-DINOv2 94.9); ATRNet-STAR SOC-50 10-shot frozen iTPN-B ablation: pixel target 32.9 %, fused multi-scale target 39.2 %; FUSAR-Ship 30 % train: iTPN-B 93.0, SARMAE 92.9, SAMBA 85.0. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)
- Inkawhich 2025: ATRNet-SOC40 few-shot with [10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500] shots per class, 10 splits; average NN accuracy across MSTAR/SAMPLE/ATRNet tasks: AFRL-DINOv2-p3/p4 79.6 vs SARATR-X 43.2-44.6 vs off-the-shelf DINOv2/DINOv3/PE-Core 25.6-34.4. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)
- NUDT4MSTAR / ATRNet-STAR dataset paper (arXiv 2501.13354, Jan 2025): 194,324 chips of 128 px at 0.12-0.15 m, 40 types / 21 classes / 4 categories, targets randomly offset (non-centred); SARATR-X SOC-40 96.4 / SOC-50 85.2 vs HiViT 86.8 / 68.0; EOC-Scene (train simple backgrounds, test urban/factory/woodland) very low: ViT 12.9, HDANet 33.6, woodland SARATR-X 2.7; models "have difficulty distinguishing between targets and complex background clutter"; transfer of SOC-40 weights to other SAR chip datasets at 5-shot "would greatly benefit" vehicles / aircraft but "the transfer effect is not obvious for the ship target class dataset in the sea scene". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13354v1)
- SAMBA (arXiv 2606.31668): per search snippet, HiViT + SARATR-X at 5/10/20-shot 76.5 / 80.8 / 85.1 in its backbone table; MSTAR comparison includes 1-shot, 2-shot and 30 % settings (table truncated). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31668)
- BIDFC (TGRS 2021 / arXiv 2408.03627): 91.25 % MSTAR with 3.13 % of training data fine-tuned; 90.13 % linear. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03627)
- Awais et al., "Cross-Dataset Transfer and Unknown-Class Detection in Imbalanced SAR Ship Classification", IMTA-X @ ICPR 2026 (arXiv 2609.31658): six frozen pretrained backbones; OpenSARShip -> FUSARShip transfer "64.5% accuracy but 33.3% balanced accuracy" (near chance); in-domain best balanced accuracy from SARDet100K-pretrained backbone (73.4 % FUSARShip, 53.2 % OpenSARShip). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31658)
- Optical, Agrawal et al. 2026: at 1 % labels SatDINO leads DINO by 8.9 (RSICD) and 4.4 (UC Merced); Scale-MAE leads MAE by 15.4 (RSICD); RemoteCLIP leads CLIP by 4.5 (RESISC45); DINOv3-SAT trails by 46.1 (UC Merced) and 28.2 (RESISC45). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849)
- PANGAEA 10 % labels: UNet vs CROMA on AI4SmallFarms 34.84 vs 38.48; on SpaceNet 7 46.08 vs 42.15; no 1 % setting. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204v1)
- VHR-Bench 10 % labels: SkySense "leads when labels are scarce" (65.8 vs web DINOv3 64.0). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)

### Inferences
- Published SAR chip gains at low shots are tens of points, but every strong result starts from ImageNet/DINOv2 weights and uses a pretraining pool that already contains target chips (SARATR-X: 46k vehicle chips; AFRL-DINOv2-p3: ATRNet-SOC40 train, unlabeled; SARATR-X-v2: ATRNet-STAR train). None isolates "scene-only pool -> chip task". Our SARDet-only and FAIR-CSAR-only pools are a cleaner test of scene-to-chip transfer than any published SAR foundation model.
- The ship-sea-scene exception in the ATRNet-STAR paper ("transfer effect is not obvious for the ship target class dataset in the sea scene") hints that clutter context mediates transfer, consistent with region-centred views mattering.

### Gaps
- No paper reports SAR chip results as 1 % / 10 % label fractions directly comparable to ours; the field uses k-shot (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40) or 3.13 % / 30 %. SARATR-X-v2 and SAMBA full few-shot tables were not readable in the fetched windows.
- The Sensors 2026 hierarchical-SSL paper (10.3390/s26010122) was blocked (HTTP 403); its abstract says SSL on measured SAMPLE data alone beats synthetic-augmented baselines, no ImageNet numbers seen. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/1/122)

## Key question 5: 2025-2026 papers on scene-to-chip transfer / "pretrain on scenes, fine-tune on targets" / domain gap between pretraining scenes and target chips (SAR or aerial)

### Takeaway
Scene-and-chip mixing is now standard in SAR foundation models (SARATR-X 186k, SARATR-X-v2 320k, XuanJian-DINOSAR 360k, SARMAE 1M, AFRL-DINOv2 110k), but none of them analyses the scene-vs-chip composition or reports what happens when the pool is scenes only. The nearest statements are SARATR-X's open issue on "automatic target slicing" from larger scenes, Inkawhich's evidence that satellite-domain DINOv3 transfers worse than web DINOv3 to SAR chips, and T-SAR-JEPA's staged sensor adaptation. No 2025-2026 paper was found that frames "pretraining scenes vs target chips" as a domain gap and measures it.

### Cited findings

- Wu et al., XuanJian-DINOSAR, arXiv 2611.06105 (4 Nov 2026): "over 360K publicly available SAR target chips, detection images, and scene images"; adapts optically pretrained DINOv2 with DINO + iBOT + KoLeo plus a SAR local consistency branch (intensity transforms, Gamma multiplicative speckle, "a cosine consistency loss aligns patch tokens at corresponding locations"); claims SOTA "on most evaluation metrics"; no breakdown of chips vs scenes, no scene/chip mismatch discussion (abstract only; v1 HTML returned 404). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06105)
- Liu et al., SARMAE, CVPR 2026 (arXiv 2512.16635, Dec 2025 / Mar 2026): "SAR-1M, the first million-scale SAR dataset, with additional paired optical images"; speckle-aware representation enhancement; no scene/chip or negative-transfer discussion in abstract. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16635)
- SARATR-X-v2 (Sep 2026): 17-dataset 320K pool (M4-SAR 56,116 at 512 px; ATRNet-STAR 68,091 at 128 px; SAR-Ship 35,757 at 256 px; SSDD 1,044; plus FAIR-CSAR); test splits removed "at the image/chip level"; "Pre-training images are a mix of scenes and chips, and the paper does not analyze how that affects transfer." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)
- SARATR-X (TIP 2025): "Large SAR imagery in the detection datasets contained more targets and scene types than the annotations"; limitation: "The pre-training set image size and automatic target slicing will become important issues that need to be investigated due to the larger image size and contextual range"; "SAR targets typically exhibit a small foreground and a dynamic context range." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365)
- Inkawhich 2025: pretraining pool of ~110k images from SAR-Ships, OpenEarthMap, HRSID, FUSAR, SSDD, DualPolShips, SRSDD, CVDome, SARSIM (a scene/chip mix); adding ATRNet-SOC40-train chips (68k, unlabeled) lifts average accuracy from 61.7 (p2) to 79.6 (p3): "the AFRL-DINOv2-p3 model was the top performer because of its pretraining set". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)
- T-SAR-JEPA (arXiv 2606.05700, 2026): staged "encoder domain adaptation, temporal predictor training, and end-to-end fine-tuning"; first fine-tunes on 39,300 Capella patches to close the gap between its MSTAR / SAR-ACD pretraining data and the Capella target sensor (snippet only). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05700)
- SAFE (2024): "competes with or surpasses other state-of-the-art methods in few-shot classification and segmentation tasks, even without being trained on the sensors used for the evaluation". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00851)
- SAREO-FM (arXiv 2610.09317, Oct 2026): gains over SARMAE on MSTAR and FUSAR-Ship but "both datasets overlap with the pretraining corpus" (snippet). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09317)
- Zhang et al., CSPT ("Consecutive Pretraining", arXiv 2207.03860, 2022; TGRS): nature-scene pretraining -> SSL on unlabeled RS -> fine-tune; twelve RS datasets, optical and SAR; "almost all downstream tasks in RSD can outperform the previous method of supervised pretraining-then-fine-tuning" (abstract only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03860)
- Optical analogue of scene-to-chip: VHR-Bench continued DINOv3 on 1.02M VHR tiles: +1.2 points at 10 % labels, +0.5 at 100 %, NZ Cattle detection 59.1 -> 67.0 mAP@50 at 10 %; "does not introduce every spatial or physically grounded prior learned by VHR-native models". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)
- Wickrema et al., "Scaling Remote Sensing Foundation Models: Data Domain Tradeoffs at the Peta-Scale", arXiv 2512.23903 (Dec 2025 / Jan 2026): "performance is consistent with a data-limited regime rather than a model parameter-limited one" (abstract only; domain-tradeoff tables not seen). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23903)
- Pretraining-duration evidence relevant to finding (d): SAR-JEPA "too-long training epochs result in overfitting of Vit-Base" (200 ep default); SARATR-X "excessive training epochs often led to overfitting due to the dataset size" and final model 600 epochs on HiViT-B; AFRL-DINOv2 100 epochs; SARATR-X-v2 1200 epochs; VHR-Bench continued pretraining reported at a first-pass checkpoint of ~2,000 steps. No paper reports a steps-vs-accuracy curve crossing the ImageNet baseline. — [SAR-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15153); [SARATR-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365); [Inkawhich](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722); [SARATR-X-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238); [VHR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)

### Inferences
- The SAR foundation-model line (2024-2026) treats a bigger mixed pool as the fix and never ablates scenes-only vs chips-only; our scenes-only pools (SARDet-100K, FAIR-CSAR) and the region-centred remedy are a different axis from all of them.
- The "trained long enough" effect (finding d) has only indirect precedent: the SAR papers warn about overfitting with long training on ~100k images, which is the opposite direction, so a reported crossing point where own-scene SSL overtakes ImageNet with steps would be new. Note the VHR-Bench continued-pretraining result at only ~2,000 steps already beats the initialization, so the number of steps needed is likely recipe- and pool-dependent.
- Novelty verdict for the writer: (a) is new for SAR scene pools with a strong DINO/JEPA recipe, with optical precedent (Agrawal 2026, Corley 2024, Lahrichi 2025) that should be cited as "the same direction has been seen in optical scene classification"; (b) is new in SAR, with natural-image precedent (object-aware cropping, CAST, SoCo) and SAR-physics precedent for masking (SAMBA, scattering-guided SSL) but no label-free region proposer for views and no random-placement control; (c) replication on a second archive has no counterpart; (d) is new as a measured crossing point.
