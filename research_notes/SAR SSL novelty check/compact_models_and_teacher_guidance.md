# SSL for compact CNNs, and teacher-guided view selection vs. cheap heuristics (novelty check)

Scope: (1) self-supervised learning (SSL) on small networks (MobileNetV2/V3, EfficientNet, ResNet-18/34, ViT-Tiny) with and without distillation; (2) using a large pretrained model's attention / saliency to decide WHERE a smaller model's SSL views or masks are taken, and whether anyone compared that against cheap heuristics; (3) KD from foundation models to small models in remote sensing / SAR; (4) continued ("ImageNet-then-domain") SSL and when it hurts; (5) 2024-2026 SSL for small models in SAR / remote sensing.

Our claims to check for novelty:
(a) MobileNetV2 initialized from ImageNet weights, then DINO / LeJEPA / SparK SSL on unlabeled SAR, then fine-tuned on 1 / 3 / 10 % labels gains +21 to +35 points at 1 % labels in-domain but transfers WORSE than plain ImageNet weights when the SSL pool is a scene archive;
(b) a frozen SAR foundation model (SARMAE ViT-B) whose attention proposes the regions for the small model's SSL views does NOT beat a pixel-brightness rule on four tests, even where it finds more objects, while both beat random placement;
(c) none of this uses distillation of the teacher's features.

Date of search: 2026-11-25. Venues/years given where the source states them.

## KQ1. Compact-model SSL: what is the small-vs-large gap, which recipes close it, and did anyone start from ImageNet-supervised weights before SSL?

### Takeaway
The small-model SSL gap is well documented (2021-2023): MoCo-v2 on MobileNetV3-Large gets 36.3 % ImageNet linear vs ~71 % supervised, and the field closed it two ways: distillation from a big SSL teacher (SEED, CompRess, DisCo, BINGO, SimReg, RoB, D-MAE-Tiny) or, without distillation, changing the view-sampling recipe (SSLight, ICLR 2023: MobileNetV2 DINO 66.2 -> 68.3, on par with KD methods). Every paper found starts the small network from random initialization on ImageNet; none starts from ImageNet-supervised weights and then does SSL on a new domain. Our "ImageNet-init -> SSL on SAR" recipe for MobileNetV2 is therefore not covered by this literature.

### Cited Findings

**The gap itself**
- SEED (Fang et al., ICLR 2021): "contrastive methods work well for large models but not for small ones"; SEED raises ImageNet-1k linear top-1 from 42.2 % to 67.6 % on EfficientNet-B0 and from 36.3 % to 68.2 % on MobileNetV3-Large relative to MoCo-v2 baselines, by having the student match the teacher's similarity distribution over a queue of instances. — [arXiv 2101.04731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04731v2); [ICLR 2021 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2707)
- SSLight (Tan, Saleh, Martinez, "Effective self-supervised pre-training on low-compute networks without distillation", ICLR 2023, Samsung AI Cambridge): supervised vs baseline SSL linear top-1 on ImageNet — MobileNetV2 71.9 supervised vs 60.6 (MoCo-v2 multi-view), 65.2 (SwAV), 66.2 (DINO); ResNet18 69.8 vs 62.2 (DINO); ResNet34 73.3 vs 67.7 (DINO); ViT-Ti/16 72.2 vs 66.7 (DINO). — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02808v2)
- SSLight's central claim: "there is no intrinsic architectural bottleneck"; the gap is a "trade-off between the model complexity and the regularization strength" of the view-sampling (augmentation) policy, which is designed for large networks and is too demanding for small ones. — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)

**Recipes that close the gap WITHOUT distillation**
- SSLight recipe (change the local-view / multi-crop sampling): MobileNetV2 DINO 66.2 -> 68.3 (+2.1), SwAV 65.2 -> 67.3 (+2.1), MoCo-v2 60.6 -> 61.6 (+1.0); ResNet18 DINO 62.2 -> 65.7 (+3.5); ResNet34 67.7 -> 69.7 (+2.0); ViT-Ti/16 66.7 -> 69.5 (+2.8); ViT-Ti/32 55.4 -> 60.1 (+4.7); ResNet50 73.4 -> 73.4 (+0.0); ViT-S/16 75.4 -> 76.1 (+0.7). "The smaller the network the larger the improvement." — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- SSLight pilot: MobileNetV2 MoCo-v2 goes from 53.8 (two views) to 60.6 (multi-crop), +6.8, i.e. local views alone are a large part of the small-model fix. Best global/local crop scale ranges and best global-vs-local loss weight both fall in [0.3, 0.4]; accuracy peaks when the mean global-to-local pixel-scale ratio is ~1.0; adding more local views saturates quickly. — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- SSLight semi-supervised ImageNet (1 % / 10 % labels): MobileNetV2 DINO 47.9 / 61.3 -> SSLight 200 ep 50.6 / 63.5 -> 400 ep 52.6 / 64.0; ResNet18 44.5 / 59.2 -> 49.8 / 63.0 -> 51.6 / 63.7; ResNet34 52.4 / 65.4 -> 55.2 / 67.2 -> 56.8 / 67.5. — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- SSLight vs KD methods, ImageNet linear top-1 (teacher in brackets): CompRess [MoCo-v2 R50] MobileNetV2 65.8 / ResNet18 62.6; SimReg [MoCo-v2 R50] 69.1 / 65.1; SEED [MoCo-v2 R152] ResNet18 59.5 / ResNet34 62.7; DisCo [R152] 65.5 / 68.1; BINGO [R152] 65.9 / 69.1; SSLight (no teacher) 200 ep: MobileNetV2 68.3 / ResNet18 65.7 / ResNet34 69.7; 400 ep: 68.8 / 66.8 / 70.8. Semi-supervised ResNet18 1 % / 10 %: SEED 44.3 / 54.8, DisCo 47.1 / 54.7, BINGO 50.3 / 61.2, SSLight 400 ep 51.6 / 63.7. — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- A Closer Look at Self-Supervised Lightweight Vision Transformers (Wang, Gao, Li, Zhang, Hu, ICML 2023): vanilla ViT-Tiny (5.7 M) reaches 79.0 % ImageNet top-1 with proper pre-training; the MAE baseline is MAE with the encoder swapped to ViT-Tiny; they add attention-map distillation from MAE-Base to get D-MAE-Tiny (released model 78.4 %). — [arXiv 2205.14443](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14443); [code mae-lite](https://github.com/wangsr126/mae-lite)

**Recipes that close the gap WITH distillation**
- DisCo (Gao et al., ECCV 2022): distills the final embedding; the extra MLP is only used during pretraining so the deployed student has no added parameters; reports +9.2 points over SEED on MobileNetV3-Large with a ResNet-50 teacher. — [arXiv 2104.09124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09124); [ECCV 2022 PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136860233.pdf)
- SimReg (Navaneet et al., 2022): plain feature regression with an MLP head; ResNet-18 student, MoCo-v2 ResNet-50 teacher: SimReg-4L-MLP 65.1 % linear vs CompRess-1q 62.6 and CompRess-2q 61.7. — [arXiv 2201.05131](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.05131)
- BINGO transfers teacher relationships by grouping similar samples into "bags"; 65.9 % ResNet-18 linear with a MoCo-v2 R152 teacher (as tabulated in SSLight). — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- Replace one Branch (RoB; Duval, Misra, Ballas, Meta, arXiv Jan 2023): swap one branch of a joint-embedding SSL method for a frozen large SSL teacher (DINO, SwAV, iBOT, MSN); best ViT-Tiny improves over prior SSL SOTA on ImageNet by 2.3 %; ViT-Tiny students on par with or better than supervised-distilled DeiT on iNaturalist, CIFAR, Clevr/Count, Clevr/Dist, Places. — [arXiv 2301.09451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09451)
- Retro (follow-up, arXiv 2405.15311) reports that a ResNet-50x2 teacher with an 8192-d embedding "drops significantly on MobileNet-v3-Large and EfficientNet-B1", i.e. distillation gains are less consistent for MobileNet-family students than for ResNet-18. — [arXiv 2405.15311](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15311v2)

**Initialization**
- SSLight: models are "pre-trained from scratch" on ImageNet-1K without any distillation signal; no ImageNet-supervised initialization is described. — [ar5iv 2210.02808](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)
- RoB abstract/page: initialization not stated; the method is pure ImageNet SSL with a teacher. — [arXiv 2301.09451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09451)
- SEED, DisCo, SimReg: all are ImageNet-1k pretraining of randomly initialized students with a teacher (per their abstracts). — [SEED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04731v2); [DisCo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09124); [SimReg](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.05131)

### Inferences
- The literature's "small-model gap" is an ImageNet-from-scratch phenomenon. Our setting (MobileNetV2 already at ImageNet-supervised quality, then SSL on SAR) sidesteps the regime these papers study; the published fixes (SSLight view sampling, KD) are complementary, not prior art for our (a) and (c).
- SSLight's view-sampling result (local-view scale and global-to-local pixel ratio matter most for small nets) is the closest published mechanism to our "placement / crop size matters" findings and should be cited as the reason WHERE and HOW BIG views are sampled is expected to matter for MobileNetV2.
- Our in-domain gains at 1 % labels (+21 to +35 points) are much larger than the +2.7 to +5.3 point 1 %-label gains SSLight reports on ImageNet, which is consistent with SAR being far from ImageNet and the ImageNet baseline being weak at 1 % on SAR; no paper found reports the comparable number for a MobileNet on radar.

### Gaps
- No paper found in which a compact CNN is initialized from ImageNet-supervised weights and then SSL-pretrained on a new domain (queries: "ImageNet initialized self-supervised pretraining MobileNetV2 target domain few labels", "continued self-supervised pretraining ImageNet initialized domain-specific hurts transfer small CNN"). Closest are ResNet-50 (HPT, Ciga & Xu) and ViT-S/Tiny (Mine-JEPA), see KQ2.
- RoB's per-architecture tables (ResNet-18 / MobileNet numbers) were not extracted; only the abstract page was readable (PDF fetch returned binary).
- CompRess, BINGO original papers not fetched directly; their numbers are taken from SSLight's comparison table, which SSLight notes use different teachers and epochs per method.

## KQ2. "ImageNet-initialized then SSL on a new domain" (continued / intermediate pretraining) for small CNNs: when does it hurt?

### Takeaway
Hierarchical Pre-Training (Reed et al., WACV 2022) is the canonical ImageNet-SSL-then-target-SSL study (ResNet-50, with ResNet-18/BYOL in the appendix); it finds continued SSL almost always helps the target task and converges 80x faster, but it never tests whether the continued-pretrained model transfers back to other datasets, which is exactly our negative finding (a). The 2026 Mine-JEPA sonar paper shows that in-domain SSL from an ImageNet start can hurt (DINOv3 -10 to -13 points after in-domain SSL; BYOL below ImageNet-only), and the Sentinel-2 study (2025) shows in-domain SSL often only ties or loses to ImageNet at 64-1024 labels. GFM (ICCV 2023) adds an explicit ImageNet-feature distillation term precisely because plain continual pretraining gave only small gains. No one reports the cross-archive "transfers worse than ImageNet" effect for a MobileNet-class CNN on SAR.

### Cited Findings
- HPT (Reed et al., "Self-Supervised Pretraining Improves Self-Supervised Pretraining", WACV 2022): MoCo-v2, ResNet-50 (appendix: BYOL and ResNet-18); 16 datasets incl. aerial (xView, RESISC, UC-Merced), medical (CheXpert, Chest-X-ray-kids), driving, DomainNet. Base = 800-ep MoCo-v2 ImageNet. HPT converges by ~5k steps where target-only pretraining needed up to 400k steps (80x) on 15 of 16 datasets; target-only beat HPT only on DomainNet Quickdraw (largest domain gap). HPT overfits after ~5k steps, more so on small datasets. "HPT-BN consistently outperforms Target pretraining with only 1 % of images." — [ar5iv 2103.12718](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718); [WACV 2022 page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/html/Reed_Self-Supervised_Pretraining_Improves_Self-Supervised_Pretraining_WACV_2022_paper.html)
- HPT detection numbers (Pascal VOC07 AP): target-only 48.4, base 57.0, HPT base->target 57.1, HPT-BN 57.5; BDD AP: 24.3 / 27.1 / 28.1 / 28.0; COCO box AP: 36.0 / 38.0 / 38.4 / 38.2. UC-Merced linear: base 98.2 %, HPT 99.0 %. — [ar5iv 2103.12718](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718)
- HPT with a SUPERVISED ImageNet base (Appendix C.2): matched or beat target/base linear eval on 13 of 16 datasets but often needed 50k rather than 5k iterations; target-only beat HPT-sup on BDD and CheXpert, with a larger gap on Quickdraw; authors conclude supervised pretraining helps less when the domain gap is large; augmentation robustness was weaker with the supervised base on BDD and CheXpert. — [ar5iv 2103.12718](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718)
- HPT does NOT test whether HPT models transfer to other datasets worse than the base model. — [ar5iv 2103.12718](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718)
- Ciga & Xu ("Resource and data efficient self supervised learning", 2021): second-stage SSL pretraining of an already ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 on a new domain improves over both SSL-only and ImageNet-only initializations across several domains, even with limited pretraining data. — [arXiv 2109.01721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01721)
- GFM (Mendieta et al., "Towards Geospatial Foundation Models via Continual Pretraining", ICCV 2023): starts from ImageNet-22k Swin and continues pretraining (SimMIM-based) on GeoPile; "standard continual pretraining gave only small gains"; using ImageNet representations as an auxiliary distillation objective during pretraining gave the stronger model; evaluated on seven downstream datasets. — [arXiv 2302.04476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04476); [ICCV 2023 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Mendieta_Towards_Geospatial_Foundation_Models_via_Continual_Pretraining_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- Lahrichi et al., "Is Self-Supervised Pre-training on Satellite Imagery Better than ImageNet? A Systematic Study with Sentinel-2" (arXiv Feb 2025; ResNet-50 SwAV and ViT-B MAE on 1.23 M Sentinel-2 RGB images): "pre-training with remote sensing data does not offer consistent improvements in downstream performance"; "the presumed benefits of SSL pre-training on RS data may be overstated". Linear-probe examples at 1024 labels: MAE SustainBench IN 0.30 vs GeoNet 0.27; LandCoverNet 0.42 vs 0.40; SwAV EuroSAT 0.94 vs 0.92; ties on DeepGlobe/EuroSAT. Two-stage IN->GeoNet beat GeoNet-only on 5 of 6 tasks but by only ~1-2 %. RGB only, no SAR, no small backbones. — [arXiv 2502.10669](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10669v1)
- Mine-JEPA (Kwon et al., Maum AI, arXiv Apr 2026; ViT-S/16 21.6 M and ViT-Tiny 5.5 M; ImageNet-1K init then in-domain SIGReg/LeJEPA-style SSL on 153 K side-scan-sonar patches from 1,170 images): 3-class macro-F1 0.820 (ViT-S) / 0.814 (ViT-Tiny) vs DINOv3 0.810 vs ImageNet-only 0.739. In-domain SSL HURTS in several configurations: applying SIGReg to DINOv3 drops macro-F1 0.810 -> 0.706 (-10.4) with real data and -> 0.677 (-13.3) with synthetic added; BYOL with real+synthetic scores 0.693, below the ImageNet-only 0.739; natural-image augmentations during SSL drop random-init performance to 0.312, below 0.557 for random init with no pretraining at all. ImageNet init before in-domain SSL beats random init before in-domain SSL: 0.756 vs 0.725 (+3.1). No reduced-label-fraction experiments. — [arXiv 2604.00383](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00383)
- Medical: Kalapos & Gyires-Tóth (2D medical segmentation) found that in low-data scenarios supervised ImageNet pretraining achieves the best accuracy, needing <100 annotated samples. — [arXiv 2209.00314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00314)
- Medical: diabetic-retinopathy study reports SSL models "show further benefits to supervised models when initialized from ImageNet". — [PMC9578637](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9578637/)
- SAR (large models): Inkawhich, "On the Status of Foundation Models for SAR Imagery" (AFRL, arXiv Sep 2025): self-supervised fine-tuning of public DINOv2 ViT-B on SAR ("AFRL-DINOv2s", start from torch.hub weights) reaches ~79.6 % mean nearest-neighbour accuracy over 10 tasks vs ~29.5 % for off-the-shelf DINOv2 ViT-B and beats SARATR-X; MSTAR SOC/EOC kNN: SARATR-X-ckpt800 99.3/82.8, AFRL-p3 98.9/90.7, AFRL-p4 99.0/92.4. ATRNet-SOC40 10-shot LoRA: AFRL-p3 51.7, AFRL-p1 22.1, torch.hub DINOv2 14.8, SARATR-X 13.2; 100-shot: 82.7 / 60.5 / 61.5 / 52.1 (caveat: p3 saw unlabeled ATRNet-SOC40 train images during pretraining). Satellite-pretrained DINOv3-Sat did worse than web DINOv3 on SAR tasks, attributed to satellite data favouring scene over object features. No small-model or distillation experiments; smallest models are SAR-SimCLR (~11 M) and ResNet-50. — [arXiv 2509.21722](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722v1)
- SAR-JEPA (Li et al., arXiv 2311.15153): "owing to the large difference between SAR and RGB images, ImageNet weights cannot easily obtain effective features with small samples when fine-tuning"; MAE pretrained on SAR beats ImageNet init after fine-tuning; few-shot reported as 10-shot MSTAR, not label fractions; large backbones. — [arXiv 2311.15153](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15153v5)

### Inferences
- The field has strong evidence that ImageNet-init + in-domain SSL helps the in-domain task (HPT, Ciga & Xu, Mine-JEPA, AFRL-DINOv2s) and emerging evidence that it can hurt (Mine-JEPA DINOv3 and BYOL cases; Sentinel-2 ties/losses), but nobody evaluated the "pretrain on archive A, fine-tune on dataset B" cross-archive transfer for a small CNN, and nobody reports it for SAR. Our finding (a) — in-domain gain, cross-archive loss vs ImageNet — is a gap. The nearest analogue is Inkawhich's note that DINOv3-Sat (scene-level satellite SSL) transfers to SAR targets worse than web DINOv3, which matches our "scene archive -> target chips" direction.
- GFM's decision to distill ImageNet features during continual pretraining is implicit evidence that plain continued SSL erodes ImageNet transferability; it is the mechanism one would cite to explain our cross-archive loss.
- Mine-JEPA is the closest paper to our recipe (ImageNet init, LeJEPA-family objective, small ViT, tiny sonar domain) and reports the same two qualitative facts: ImageNet init before in-domain SSL beats random init, and in-domain SSL is not safe by default. It uses ViTs, not CNNs, and no label-fraction sweep.

### Gaps
- No paper found reporting "SSL on domain archive A hurts transfer to domain dataset B relative to ImageNet weights" for a compact CNN; queries tried: "self-supervised pretraining in-domain remote sensing transfers worse than ImageNet small labels negative transfer SAR", "continued self-supervised pretraining ImageNet initialized domain-specific hurts transfer small CNN".
- HPT's ResNet-18/BYOL appendix numbers not extracted (page summary only mentioned them).
- GFM's exact continual-pretraining-without-distillation vs with-distillation numbers not extracted (abstract-level only).

## KQ3. Teacher-guided view / mask selection: attention, CAM, saliency or features used to choose crops or masks for SSL of another (smaller) model; was a cheap heuristic ever found equal?

### Takeaway
Guided masking (AttMask, SemMAE, HPM, AutoMAE, ADIOS) and guided cropping (object-aware cropping, CAST, saliency-constrained crops, "Coarse is Better") all report gains over random, typically +1 to +3 points, but in nearly every case the guide comes from the model itself (EMA teacher) or a supervised reference, not from a frozen larger foundation model guiding a smaller student. The one direct heuristic-vs-learned comparison found ("Coarse Is Better?", 2023) shows an almost-random grid crop beats ground-truth object boxes and nearly matches learned proposals; the saliency-crop paper (Addepalli et al. 2022) shows the gain disappears once the cost of the saliency model is charged to the budget. No paper found compares a foundation-model attention proposer against a pixel-brightness or edge heuristic for a small CNN's SSL views. Our finding (b) appears novel in its specific form but is consistent with the "coarse objectness is enough" result.

### Cited Findings

**Attention / saliency-guided MASKING (MIM)**
- AttMask (Kakogeorgiou et al., ECCV 2022): teacher = EMA of the student (same ViT-S/16), attention from the teacher's last layer averaged over heads; masks the MOST attended tokens. iBOT, 20 % of ImageNet-1k, 100 ep, k-NN / linear: random block-wise 46.7 / 56.4; random 10-50 % 47.8 / 56.7; AttMask-Low (mask least-attended) 44.0 / 53.4; AttMask-Hint 49.5 / 57.5; AttMask-High 49.7 / 57.9. Full ImageNet 100 ep: iBOT 71.5 / 74.4 -> AttMask-High 72.5 / 75.7 -> AttMask-Hint 72.8 / 76.1. With DINO (k-NN only) the effect is near zero: none 43.0, random 43.4, Low 42.7, Hint 43.6, High 43.5. Low-shot k-NN at 1 label/class: iBOT 32.9 vs AttMask-High 37.1. Explanation: masking non-salient regions gives a weak signal; masking salient ones "creates the more aggressive task of reconstructing the most salient regions and guides the model to explore the other regions". — [ar5iv 2203.12719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12719); [ECCV 2022 PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136900299.pdf)
- SemMAE (Li et al., NeurIPS 2022): masks by learned semantic parts rather than random patches; 84.5 % ImageNet fine-tune, +1.4 over vanilla MAE; needs a hand-designed easy-to-hard schedule with a fixed number of parts. — [ar5iv 2206.10207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.10207)
- Hard Patches Mining (Wang et al., CVPR 2023): the model predicts per-patch reconstruction loss and masks the highest-loss patches on an easy-to-hard schedule, mixing loss-based and random masks; high-loss patches tend to be discriminative, so objects are almost entirely hidden. — [CVPR 2023 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Wang_Hard_Patches_Mining_for_Masked_Image_Modeling_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf); [ar5iv 2304.05919](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.05919)
- AutoMAE (Chen et al., PRCV 2023): adversarial Gumbel-softmax mask generator; MAE-800 66.7 % linear / 83.32 % fine-tune, described as "competitive" with random masking. — [arXiv 2303.06583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06583v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/hyperea/automae)
- ADIOS learns the mask adversarially with an extra U-Net masking module. — [ar5iv 2303.06583 related-work](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.06583)
- MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022): among the mask-sampling strategies tested (random, block, grid), random sampling worked best. — [arXiv 2111.06377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377)
- Prior-knowledge-guided attention in SSL ViTs (arXiv 2209.03745, 2022) exists as a related line (attention guided by priors) but was not fetched. — [arXiv 2209.03745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03745)

**Attention / saliency / proposal-guided CROPPING (joint-embedding SSL)**
- Object-aware cropping (Mishra et al., TMLR 2022): replaces one or both random crops with crops from an unsupervised object proposal method (LOD); on OpenImages with MoCo-v2, +8.8 % mAP over random scene-level cropping; gains on COCO and VOC. Random scene-level cropping is argued to hurt contrastive SSL on non-curated (multi-object) data. — [arXiv 2112.00319](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00319)
- CAST (Selvaraju et al., "CASTing Your Model: Learning to Localize Improves Self-Supervised Representations", CVPR 2021): random crops constrained so each covers at least a fraction φ of an unsupervised saliency map; φ = 0 recovers plain MoCo random cropping. — [arXiv 2012.04630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04630)
- Addepalli, Bhogale, Dey, Venkatesh Babu, "Towards Efficient and Effective Self-Supervised Learning of Visual Representations" (IISc, arXiv 2022): Grad-CAM saliency from a SUPERVISED ImageNet-pretrained reference model; accept a random crop only if its mean saliency exceeds the full-image mean (retry 10x, then random). BYOL 50 ep on ImageNet-100 (backbone likely ResNet-18): random crops 63.64 % -> 25 % good crops 64.50 -> 50 % 66.30 -> 100 % saliency crops 66.72 (+3.08). Caveat in their own supplement: BYOL at 100 ep without saliency reaches 71.02 %, which is higher once the 90-epoch supervised reference model is charged to the budget. No center-crop, center-bias, or edge-detector heuristic is compared; unsupervised saliency (DINO/BAS-Net) is mentioned only as a possibility, not tested. — [ar5iv 2210.09866](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.09866)
- "Coarse Is Better? A New Pipeline Towards SSL with Uncurated Images" (Zhu, He, Wu, Nanjing Univ., arXiv 2306.04244, 2023): VOC2007 detection after 800-ep ResNet-50 pretraining on crops, AP50 / mAP: BYOL Image-Crop 63.3 / 63.0, GT-box crop 66.9 / 69.2, almost-random Grid-Crop 68.1 / 70.3, learned Our-Crop 69.5 / 70.7; MoCo-v2: 61.8 / 62.2, 62.3 / 66.2, 65.6 / 67.7, 67.2 / 69.6. Selective Search gives BYOL 68.0 AP50, EdgeBox 68.1 (vs Grid 68.1, Our-Crop 69.5). Statement: "the almost random 'Grid' crop is even consistently better than the 'GT' strategy." Explanation: crops need only COARSELY contain objects (partial or multiple objects are fine); both very high objectness (GT) and very low objectness (Poor-Crop) hurt. The objectness-scoring backbone stage is "not strictly necessary" (Table 11). — [ar5iv 2306.04244](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)

**A larger/smaller model's attention used as a crop proposer (no SSL training of the student)**
- A² ("Smaller Self-Supervised ViTs Localize Better than Larger Ones", Rammohan, Ha, Vondrick, arXiv June 2026, listed as NeurIPS 2026): a SMALL self-supervised ViT's last-layer attention proposes crops around attention peaks and a LARGER ViT embeds them; entirely frozen features, no student SSL training; reported competitive with DFR and better than end-to-end attention training under distribution shift on 5 benchmarks. Localization quality of DINO/DINOv2/DINOv3/MAE/iBOT attention DEGRADES with model size; OpenCLIP does not. Abstract does not report random/center-crop baselines. — [arXiv 2606.03148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03148); [NeurIPS 2026 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/154201)

### Inferences
- The direction of our setup (big frozen SAR foundation model -> proposes views -> small CNN trains with DINO/LeJEPA/SparK, no feature distillation) is the inverse of A² (small proposes, big embeds) and different from AttMask/HPM/AutoMAE (the model guides itself via EMA or an adversary). No found paper matches "frozen foundation-model attention as proposer for a smaller student's SSL views".
- The empirical pattern in the literature supports our null: "Coarse Is Better" shows near-random coarse crops match learned objectness proposals and beat precise object boxes; Addepalli et al. show saliency-crop gains are small (+3) and vanish under matched compute. A pixel-brightness rule on SAR (bright scatterers = targets) is a near-ideal "coarse objectness" heuristic, so finding it equal to SARMAE attention is consistent with these results, and the "both beat random" part matches object-aware cropping's +8.8 mAP over random scene crops on uncurated data.
- AttMask's DINO result (no gain from attention-guided masking without a MIM loss) is relevant: guided selection helps when the pretext task is reconstruction, less so for pure joint-embedding objectives; our DINO/LeJEPA arms may be in that regime.

### Gaps
- No paper found that compares a learned/foundation-model proposer against brightness, edge, or center heuristics for SSL view placement and reports equality; queries tried: "attention-guided crops self-supervised learning teacher attention selects views small model", "object-centric crops self-supervised learning saliency proposals versus random crop ContraCAM object-aware cropping results", "learning where to mask AutoMAE random masking competitive attention-guided masking ablation". The closest is Grid vs GT vs Our-Crop vs Selective Search vs EdgeBox in "Coarse Is Better".
- ContraCAM (Mo et al., NeurIPS 2021, object-aware contrastive learning via CAM) did not appear in results; not verified here.
- MST (masked self-supervised transformer, NeurIPS 2021, masks low-attention tokens) is referenced by AttMask as the opposite strategy but was not fetched; no numbers.
- ADIOS and SemMAE exact random-vs-guided ablation tables were not extracted.
- A² full paper not fetched; whether it has a random/center-crop baseline is unknown.

## KQ4. Knowledge distillation from a foundation model to a small model in remote sensing / SAR; has anyone compared "teacher tells where to look" with "teacher features distilled"?

### Takeaway
Remote-sensing KD work in 2024-2026 distills features or relations from a supervised detector or a geospatial foundation model (TerraMind-B -> TerraMind-S radar-only, NeurIPS 2026: +11.1 and +2.3 mIoU; SURGE SAR ship detection, 2026: ResNet-18 student 62.9 -> 68.0 mAP on SSDD, beating its ResNet-101 teacher). None of them isolates a "teacher proposes regions only" condition against "teacher features distilled"; no paper found distills a SAR foundation model (SARATR-X, SARMAE) into a MobileNet-class classifier.

### Cited Findings
- "Generalised Distillation of Geospatial Foundation Models" (Amina Said, NeurIPS 2026): frozen multimodal TerraMind-B teacher (optical + radar) -> lightweight radar-only TerraMind-S student via offline feature-based KD; mIoU +11.11 % on Crop Type Mapping South Sudan and +2.29 % on Sen1Floods11 over TerraMind's radar-only SOTA; "later layers matter more"; distillation benefit depends on task. Abstract gives no no-KD student baseline and no attention/saliency guidance. — [NeurIPS 2026 page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/163753)
- SURGE, "Lightweight SAR Ship Detection via Contrastive Distillation" (Devasundaram, Saber Latibari, Mahalanobis, Univ. of Arizona, GLSVLSI 2026): relation-aware InfoNCE distillation from a ResNet-101 teacher to a ResNet-18 student; Faster R-CNN SSDD mAP teacher 66.70, student no-KD 62.87, student + SURGE 68.03 (beats teacher by 1.33); HRSID 62.09 / 59.93 / 65.96. For RetinaNet and DETR the student without KD already equals the teacher and KD gives ≤0.7 points (RetinaNet SSDD 61.12 -> 60.33, i.e. slightly worse). Baseline MGD (reconstruct teacher features from randomly masked student features) scores 62.80 / 58.70 / 37.36. No region-selection ablation, no random-teacher baseline. — [arXiv 2605.30380](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30380)
- DIPKD (AAAI 2025): SAR object detection KD with a Selective Noise Suppression module to handle speckle before transfer; student beats teacher by 1.3-2.9 % on some metrics. — [AAAI 33004](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33004/35159)
- DTKDP (arXiv 2609.24872, 2026): dual-teacher KD + pruning for lightweight oriented SAR ship detection; student trained with ground-truth plus two supervised teacher branches. — [arXiv 2609.24872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24872)
- Survey: "Applications of Knowledge Distillation in Remote Sensing: A Survey" (arXiv 2409.12111, 2024). — [arXiv 2409.12111](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12111)
- HyperKD (DSAA 2025): distils Prithvi (ViT MAE geospatial FM) for EnMAP hyperspectral data. — [arXiv 2508.09453](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09453v1)
- RS-MTDF (2025): multi-teacher distillation from frozen vision foundation models for semi-supervised RS segmentation. — [arXiv 2506.08772](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08772v2)
- SARATR-X (Li et al., arXiv 2405.09365, 2024-2025): SAR foundation model trained with masked image modeling on 0.18 M unlabeled SAR target samples; no compressed/lightweight variant is described. — [arXiv 2405.09365](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365v5)
- Inkawhich 2025: no distillation or small-model experiments in the SAR foundation-model status report. — [arXiv 2509.21722](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722v1)

### Inferences
- "Teacher proposes where to look, student trains with its own SSL, no feature distillation" is absent from the RS/SAR KD literature found; every SAR paper transfers features, relations, or logits. Our (c) is thus a different axis from all of these, and our (b) null is the first measurement of that axis found.
- SURGE's RetinaNet/DETR rows (student already equal to teacher, KD adds nothing) is useful precedent that teacher-derived signal adds little when the student is already near ceiling on small SAR ship datasets.

### Gaps
- No paper found distilling a SAR foundation model into a MobileNet / EfficientNet classifier (queries: "SAR foundation model distillation lightweight student SARATR-X ship ATR self-supervised 2025 2026", "knowledge distillation remote sensing foundation model lightweight student SAR 2024 2025").
- No paper found comparing "where to look" guidance vs feature distillation under matched compute in any domain.
- TerraMind distillation absolute numbers and no-KD student baseline not available (login-gated page).

## Summary for the novelty argument (plain words)

- What is already known: small networks do badly under SSL from scratch on ImageNet (MobileNetV3 36 % vs 72 % supervised); fixes are a big teacher (SEED/DisCo/BINGO/SimReg/RoB) or gentler local-view sampling (SSLight). Starting a small net from ImageNet weights and then doing SSL on a new domain is not studied in that literature; it is studied for ResNet-50 (HPT) and small ViTs (Mine-JEPA), where it helps in-domain and sometimes hurts.
- What nobody measured: whether the in-domain-SSL-from-ImageNet small CNN then transfers WORSE than ImageNet to another dataset of the same modality (our (a)); whether a frozen foundation model's attention as a view proposer for a small CNN's SSL beats a brightness heuristic (our (b)); and any SAR distillation setting that transfers only "where to look" instead of features (our (c)).
- What supports our results: "Coarse Is Better" (near-random grid crops ≈ learned proposals > exact boxes), Addepalli et al. (saliency crops +3 points, gone under matched compute), AttMask (attention guidance helps MIM, not DINO), Mine-JEPA (in-domain SSL can hurt), the Sentinel-2 study (in-domain SSL often ties/loses to ImageNet), and GFM (needs ImageNet distillation to keep transfer).
