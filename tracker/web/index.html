What has been run, and what it says

Every row of this study is one recipe: unlabeled pool for self-supervised learning (SSL) → labeled set for fine-tuning → task that is scored. MobileNetV2 from ImageNet weights everywhere, 100 SSL epochs, 1 / 3 / 10 % of the labels, three splits per cell, official test sets. Numbers on this page are read from the run folders; the sentences are the current reading and are updated by hand.

no SSL or SSL on ATRNet-STAR chips or SSL on SARDet-100K scenes → fine-tune on ATRNet-STAR labels or on SARDet-100K labels → classification accuracy or detection mAP
Row labels in one line. A0 = ImageNet weights, no SSL. A1 / A2 / A3 = DINO / LeJEPA / SparK with SSL on the ATRNet chips. P4 = CondDINO (uses metadata). XA = SSL on SARDet scenes, tested on ATRNet chips (cross-dataset). XI = the RegionDINO engine on the ATRNet chips (the in-domain check, also called arm I). control = same method minus the ingredient under test. Everything else: glossary.

Reading, 2026-11-20

In-domain (SSL and test both on ATRNet chips): any SSL helps a lot at 1 % labels (21 → 35 to 41 %). CondDINO is top but uses acquisition metadata, so it sits apart. Plain DINO and LeJEPA tie. SparK is weakest here.

Cross-dataset (SSL on SARDet scenes, test on ATRNet chips): plain DINO and LeJEPA transfer worse than no SSL. RegionDINO object-only turns that into the best cross-dataset row (+9.4 over plain DINO at 1 %), with SparK second. The random-window control gets a third of the gain, so the regions matter. Cross-dataset is still far below in-domain (27 vs 35 at 1 %).

RegionDINO on the ATRNet chips themselves (the XI rows, also called arm I): no gain. Scene+object is level with plain DINO, object-only is 2 points below. A chip is already the object; cutting regions out of it only removes context. So the object views pay off when the SSL images are scenes and the test is a chip, and that is the setting to build the paper on.

Detection (SSL and test on SARDet): RegionDINO object-only is the first method to move SARDet detection by more than a point: +1.6 / +1.4 / +1.2 mAP points at 1 / 3 / 10 %, positive on all nine cells, against SparK's +0.7. But its random-window control gets the same (+1.8 / +1.6 / +1.0). So for detection the gain comes from training DINO on small object-sized crops instead of whole scenes, not from where the crops are placed. Scene+object gives half of that and its control a third.

Chip classification on SARDet itself (S1): a null. No SSL already reaches 81 % mean per-class accuracy at 1 % labels (92 % overall) and every SSL row lands within split noise of it. The six coarse classes are too easy, and the splits differ by up to 7 points, more than any method difference. Study S2 (tiles) is parked; a harder version of this test (smaller budget, or train and test on different source datasets) would be the way to get signal from chips.

Window size (ViewScale, 2026-11-22): with the object-only recipe and random square windows of one fixed size, the transfer gain over plain DINO grows from +0.6 (32 px) to +4.9 (256 px) at 1 % labels and flattens; even whole-scene windows give +4.3, so the 18-small-views recipe itself beats standard DINO crops on SAR scenes. The region-placed row stays about 5 points above the best fixed size, so placement is a second factor for scene-to-chip transfer. Detection (split 1) follows the same curve and tops out where the region-placed row sits.

A big SAR teacher as the proposer (TeacherRegions, 2026-11-22): windows placed by frozen SARMAE's attention (twice as precise on annotated objects) transfer the same or slightly worse than the brightness rule (25.3 vs 27.2 at 1 %), while still beating their own random control by 7 points. Placement matters; a better object finder does not. The cheap rule is enough. Detection split 1 agrees (teacher regions 16.9 vs rule 17.1 mAP at 1 %).

Second dataset, FAIR-CSAR (2026-11-25): a hard 22-class chip test (no SSL 24.9 / 29.2 / 39.6 mean per-class at 1 / 3 / 10 %). SSL on FAIR-CSAR's own 1 m tiles helps once it is trained as long as the SARDet runs (500 epochs): plain DINO +2.4 / +3.8 / +4.0, positive on every split at 3 and 10 %. SSL on SARDet-100K scenes hurts (-2 to -3) unless it is RegionDINO object-only, which lands above no SSL and 4 points above its DINO, like on ATRNet. With SSL on FAIR itself, RegionDINO object-only is within a point of plain DINO and beats its random-window control only at 10 % (+2.2). The SARMAE teacher as proposer finds more objects than the brightness rule (41 vs 34 %) and classifies the same (28.5 vs 28.6 at 1 %), while beating its own size-matched random control by 3 points: placement matters, a better finder does not, now on a fourth test.

FAIR backbones as a second scene archive (2026-11-26, c13): the 15 FAIR-trained backbones fine-tuned on ATRNet chips (XF rows). Plain DINO on FAIR-CSAR tiles transfers better than no SSL (+1.8 / +4.1 / +2.4 at 1 / 3 / 10 %, nine of nine pairs), where the same recipe on SARDet-100K scenes gave -3.0 / -1.6 / +0.5. So "scene SSL hurts" is a property of the SARDet pool (nine mixed sources, mostly ships on water), not of scene SSL. RegionDINO object-only is still the best cross-dataset row from both pools (26.8 / 49.9 / 74.7 from FAIR, 27.2 / 49.0 / 73.7 from SARDet), beats plain DINO on all 18 pairs and its size-matched random control on all 18 pairs (placement +2.8 / +1.2 / +0.7 from FAIR, +6.3 / +3.6 / +1.0 from SARDet). The SARMAE teacher ties the brightness rule a fifth time (-0.4 / -0.3 / -0.3). On SARDet chips every FAIR backbone is within noise of no SSL, as every other backbone.

Next: the gradient-map proposer (GradientRegions, 12 SSL chains running on CRC), a per-pool random-window hit rate to explain why the SARDet and FAIR pools transfer so differently, then the reverse direction (SSL on ATRNet, test on FAIR / SARDet chips). Ideas and doubts go in the idea box.
