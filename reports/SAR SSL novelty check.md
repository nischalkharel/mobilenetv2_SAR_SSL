# Region-placed views remain ours; DINOSAR scooped the idea

Three of our ten findings have no published counterpart at all (scene-archive SSL transferring below ImageNet weights to another dataset's vehicle chips; a frozen SAR foundation model as region proposer being no better than a brightness rule; object views doing nothing when the SSL pool is already chips), and the other seven have partial precedent whose direction agrees with ours but whose controls, backbone, or endpoint differ. The one real threat is DINOSAR (Tang et al., International Journal of Digital Earth, accepted 4 November 2026): it trains DINO plus iBOT on 7 million SAR tiles with "content-aware multi-crop" views chosen from gradient-based content maps and shows that this beats random-crop DINO on ATRNet-STAR and SARDet-100K, which means "choose SAR SSL views by content instead of at random" is no longer a claim we can own ([DINOSAR README](https://github.com/kenanking/DINOSAR); [results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kenanking/DINOSAR/main/docs/results.md)). What DINOSAR does not do, and what nobody else does, is run a random-placement control with the same window sizes, drop the whole-scene views, test a scene-only pool against ImageNet initialization, sweep window size separately from placement, or use a compact CNN; those are exactly the pieces that turn our result from "content-aware crops help" into "where you cut the views decides whether scene-archive SSL helps or hurts a small model on object chips". SAMBA's scatterer-guided masking (brightness above mean plus 1.2 standard deviations) is the closest SAR precedent for our brightness rule, but it steers which patches a masked autoencoder hides on chips, not where DINO's views are cut on scenes ([SAMBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31668v2)). The research is ready for a workshop or a second-tier venue as it stands; a main-track conference paper needs a DINOSAR-style content-map proposer as a baseline, a large SAR foundation model fine-tuned under our exact 1 / 3 / 10 % protocol, the matched-steps comparison, and an explanation of why random windows recover a third of the gain on ATRNet-STAR but most of it on FAIR-CSAR.

## DINOSAR cut views by content first, but never ran our controls

DINOSAR is the paper a reviewer will put next to ours, so the differences have to be exact. Its recipe is DINO plus iBOT plus a KoLeo regulariser, trained for 60 epochs on UniSAR-7M, a pool of **7,047,666 non-overlapping 256 by 256 tiles** from five public datasets and 2,274 commercial Capella, ICEYE and Umbra scenes, with ViT-S/16 (21.4M parameters) and ViT-B/16 (85.3M) backbones ([DINOSAR README](https://github.com/kenanking/DINOSAR)). Its core contribution, "content-aware multi-crop", replaces random resized crops by views sampled from ratio-gradient content maps and scene-adaptive anchors, motivated by the observation that random crops "may miss target content or repeatedly sample the same strong scatterers" ([DINOSAR README](https://github.com/kenanking/DINOSAR)). The ablation that matters is small but real: with ViT-S for 5 pretraining epochs, random-crop DINO reaches **11.78 k-NN / 19.87 linear-probe accuracy on ATRNet-STAR SOC-40 and content-aware crops reach 19.76 / 22.84**; on SARDet-100K detection the full model reaches 55.28 AP against 54.22 for random-crop DINO on the same 7M pool ([DINOSAR results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kenanking/DINOSAR/main/docs/results.md)). Frozen k-NN on SOC-40 reaches 77.02 for ViT-B, far above SARATR-X at 53.24 and SARMAE at 46.92, and full fine-tuning reaches 99.1 on SOC-40 ([DINOSAR results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kenanking/DINOSAR/main/docs/results.md)). A same-group SPIE paper, CA-HLD ("context-adaptive cropping with hidden-layer distillation", October 2026), appears to extend the idea but its text could not be read ([ScholarBro listing](https://mail.scholarbro.com/p/137158-mgrl-rscc-multi-granularity-reward-reinforcement-learning-for-fine-gr)).

Our work differs on six concrete points, and each one is a point a reviewer can check. First, the proposer: DINOSAR uses gradient-magnitude saliency maps with anchors; we use connected bright regions from Felzenszwalb segmentation on pixel brightness, which yields object-sized windows rather than a density map. Second, the control: DINOSAR's baseline is standard random resized cropping, which changes both position and scale at once; our control places windows of the same size distribution at random positions, so it isolates placement from size, and no published crop-placement paper runs that control in a full factorial ([object-centric SSL survey of controls](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)). Third, the whole-scene views: DINOSAR keeps global views; we drop them and show that keeping them loses most of the gain (finding 4). Fourth, the pool: DINOSAR pretrains on 7 million mixed tiles and never isolates a scenes-only pool, so it cannot see the negative transfer we report in finding 2; its content-aware recipe is presented as a gain on top of a working baseline, not as the fix for a baseline that falls below ImageNet weights. Fifth, the backbone: ViT-S/B against our MobileNetV2, which matters because the small-model SSL literature shows crop sampling has the largest effect on the smallest networks ("the smaller the network the larger the improvement", [SSLight](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)). Sixth, the negative cases: our FAIR-CSAR result, where random windows recover most of the gain, and our chip-pool null (finding 9) cut against a simple "content-aware crops always help" story, and DINOSAR reports neither. DINOSAR also loses to SARATR-X on MSTAR 10-shot (90.7 vs 95.6), so content-aware crops are not uniformly superior even in that paper ([DINOSAR results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kenanking/DINOSAR/main/docs/results.md)).

SAMBA is the second paper to name precisely. It is a Mamba encoder of about 27M parameters and 8 GFLOPs, pretrained first with masked autoencoding on ImageNet and then on the 186K-image SARATR-X pool, and its target-awareness is a three-level **scattering-guided masking** scheme: strong scatterers are found label-free in the dB domain with an adaptive threshold of mean plus 1.2 standard deviations plus local contrast, and a 5 by 5 window counts scatterers per patch to set that patch's mask ratio ([SAMBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31668v2)). Its ablation is large: random masking versus scatter-guided masking gives 71.5 / 78.5 / 84.0 against 80.6 / 83.6 / 88.1 at 5 / 10 / 20 shots ([SAMBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31668v2)). The difference from us is categorical rather than quantitative. SAMBA's prior decides which patches of a chip a masked autoencoder must reconstruct; ours decides where on a large scene a joint-embedding method's views are cut. SAMBA operates on a chip-dominated pool and never on ATRNet-STAR; it has no random-placement control in the crop sense, no scene-to-chip test, and no comparison against a learned proposer. The brightness thresholds are close cousins, and we should cite SAMBA as the SAR-physics precedent for "bright scatterers mark targets", while noting that the same heuristic has only been used for masking, reconstruction targets (SAR-Harris in SARDet-MIM, [Remote Sensing 18:580](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-00580/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-00580.pdf)), or a half-point local-sampling gain on ship chips (PCM-SAR, 70.59 to 71.10, [PCM-SAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09502v1)). There is also a relevant warning in the masking literature: attention-guided masking helps iBOT by 1 to 2 points but gives essentially nothing to plain DINO (k-NN 43.0 to 43.6, [AttMask](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12719)), so guided selection is not guaranteed to transfer between masked and joint-embedding objectives, which is one reason our SparK exception in finding 2 deserves its own explanation.

## Scene-to-chip negative transfer is unreported in SAR but matches optical evidence

No SAR paper states that SSL on a scene archive transfers below ImageNet weights to another dataset's object chips, and the reason is structural: every SAR foundation model pools chips and scenes together and never ablates the mix. SARATR-X uses 186,600 samples from 14 chip and sliced-detection datasets ([SARATR-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365v5)); SARATR-X-v2 uses about 320K images including the 68,091 ATRNet-STAR SOC-40 training chips and 23,825 FAIR-CSAR tiles ([SARATR-X-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238v4)); XuanJian-DINOSAR uses 367,285 images that explicitly mix "target chips, detection images, and scene images" ([XuanJian-DINOSAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06105)); SARMAE uses 1.3 million images from 18 datasets ([SARMAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16635v2)). The strongest published hint that pool composition, not objective, drives chip accuracy comes from the AFRL status report: adding the unlabeled ATRNet-STAR training chips to a mostly scene and ship pool lifts average nearest-neighbour accuracy from **61.6 to 79.6**, and the author says the top model won "because of its pretraining set" ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)). The same report contains the two closest negative cases: satellite-pretrained DINOv3 (25.6) below web DINOv3 (30.8) on SAR chip tasks, explained as satellite data encouraging "scene relevant features whereas the web data encourages the learning of object relevant features", and the SAR-adapted DINOv2 without ATRNet chips trailing the untouched web DINOv2 from 100 shots upward ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)). The only paper that pretrains on SARDet-100K itself, Pu and Xu, evaluates detection on the same dataset and finds a modest +1.3 mAP over ImageNet-MAE at ViT-B (54.9 vs 53.6) ([Pu and Xu](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11249)); nobody carries a SARDet-100K-pretrained backbone to another dataset's chips.

Below-ImageNet cells do exist in SAR tables, but always framed as "ImageNet remains competitive" and never as a finding. SAR-JEPA's own table shows ImageNet-supervised linear probing on FUSAR-Ship (74.6 / 79.6 / 83.6 at 10 / 20 / 40 shots) beating every SAR-chip-pretrained masked model except SAR-JEPA itself ([SAR-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15153v6)); SARATR-X's ablation shows a SAR-only MAE ViT at 54.1 against supervised ImageNet at 58.6 on 5-shot SAR-VSA, which is why its authors recommend ImageNet weights "during initialization before pre-training on SAR datasets" ([SARATR-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365)); SSL4SAR reports 574 to 773 m glacier-front error for SSL from scratch on 9,562 Sentinel-1 images against 342 m for plain ImageNet weights ([SSL4SAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01747)). Each of these is a weak recipe or a tiny pool. Ours is a strong recipe (DINO, LeJEPA) on a 94k-scene archive that still loses, which is the version the field has not seen. The optical literature has now seen it three times in 2025 and 2026: the matched-pair study where DINOv3-SAT trails DINOv3 on every dataset and split, with losses concentrated on "classes defined by small man-made objects" and largest at 1 % labels (46.1 linear-probe points on UC Merced) ([Agrawal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849)); the Sentinel-2 study where ImageNet wins 13 of 36 linear-probe comparisons under an identical recipe ([Lahrichi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10669v1)); and the very-high-resolution benchmark where Sentinel-scale encoders transfer worst to 0.1 to 1.5 m tasks ([VHR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)). Those papers give us the explanation to cite (scene features versus object features, resolution mismatch) and show that the shape of our finding 2 is a general property of scene-centric pretraining, not a SAR oddity. The same logic covers finding 9: when the pool is already object-centred chips, there is no background to escape, and the natural-image evidence that tight object boxes are worse than loose proposals (ORL 85.4 vs 86.7; UniVIP 58.5 vs 60.2; Coarse Is Better 66.9 vs 69.5) predicts that forcing views even tighter onto a centred target gives nothing ([ORL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.11952); [UniVIP](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.06965); [Coarse Is Better](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)).

Finding 8, SSL on FAIR-CSAR's own 1 m tiles helping its chip classification only after 500 epochs, has direction precedent and no numerical precedent. Hierarchical Pre-Training established that ImageNet-then-target SSL helps the target task on 15 of 16 datasets ([HPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718)), and continued DINOv3 pretraining on a million very-high-resolution tiles adds +1.2 points at 10 % labels ([VHR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)). But no SAR paper reports a training-length curve that crosses the ImageNet baseline; the SAR papers only warn in the other direction, that "excessive training epochs often led to overfitting due to the dataset size" ([SARATR-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365)) and that "too-long training epochs result in overfitting of Vit-Base" ([SAR-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15153)). FAIR-CSAR itself has no published chip-classification result of any kind; its only SSL number is oriented detection AP50 of 42.40 for XuanJian-DINOSAR against 38.00 for an ImageNet ResNet-50 with a different backbone ([XuanJian-DINOSAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06105)). Our FAIR-CSAR chip benchmark and the 63-condition grid on it therefore have no external comparator, which is good for novelty and bad for sanity-checking, so the paper should report at least one published-style number (for example a 10-shot or full-label result) on FAIR-CSAR chips so readers can calibrate.

## Placement, size and whole-scene views: natural-image work predicts the direction, not the task dependence

Fourteen natural-image papers from 2020 to 2025 move joint-embedding views toward objects with label-free proposers, and the pattern of their gains predicts ours. Gains are large on scene-centric data and small on object-centric data: object-aware cropping gives +8.8 mAP over scene crops on OpenImages but only +0.5 top-1 on ImageNet ([Mishra et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00319v1)), and saliency-constrained crops add +2.4 VOC07 but -0.5 ImageNet ([CAST](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.04630)). SAR scenes, with small targets in large clutter, are about as scene-centric as data gets, and the background-shortcut study confirms the stakes on our test set: a ConvNeXt still scores **63.3 % on ATRNet-STAR 40-class with the vehicle removed** (chance 2.5 %), and SARATR-X gains 4 to 6 points when the vehicle is removed ([Shaya 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21827)). The placement controls that exist all point the same way as ours: SoCo's random boxes come within 0.2 AP of selective-search boxes at one proposal per image ([SoCo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.02637)); in Coarse Is Better, random boxes recover roughly half the gain of objectness crops over whole images (39.1 vs 40.2 vs 38.8 COCO AP) and an almost-random grid beats ground-truth boxes on VOC (68.1 vs 66.9) ([Coarse Is Better](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)); saliency-guided masking beats random masking by only 0.01 to 1.06 points on TinyImageNet ([Chin et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.12757)). Our "random windows of the same size get a third of the gain on ATRNet-STAR and most of it on FAIR-CSAR" is consistent with all of these and more explicit than any, because none of them matched window sizes.

Dropping the whole-scene views (finding 4) is where the literature is split and where a reviewer will push. On our side: two object crops beat object-plus-scene crops on OpenImages (58.6 vs 58.1) and on COCO detection (39.7 vs 39.4) ([Mishra et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00319v1)); MoCo trained only on ground-truth boxes is +3.35 better on cropped-box evaluation and -4.8 worse on full-image evaluation ([Purushwalkam and Gupta](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.13916)), which is the only published analogue of "placement matters for object chips, not for scene-level tasks" (our finding 6); and Coarse Is Better trains exclusively on pseudo-object crops with a CNN and beats whole-image pretraining on VOC and COCO ([Coarse Is Better](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)). Against us: the ViT literature argues global views carry the semantic signal and local crops only add diversity, with k-NN collapsing from 72.4 to 47.0 when globals are replaced by locals ([Two Global Crops](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28187v1)) and dropping local crops costing 6.8 linear points in DINO ([DINO](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.14294)), and ODIS finds that object-aware local windows are worse than random context-rich ones ([ODIS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05409)). The honest framing is that whole-scene views hurt when the downstream task is object chips cut from scene-centric data with a small CNN, and that the ViT results come from object-centric ImageNet; nobody has run object-only, scene-only and mixed views together on a cross-dataset chip task, so finding 4 stands but must be stated as task- and domain-specific.

The window-size sweep (finding 5) reproduces a known inverted-U and adds one new thing. The classification-optimal crop lower bound sits at 0.2 to 0.4 of image area across DINO (split 0.24 to 0.32), InfoMin (0.2), SSLight (both scales in [0.3, 0.4] for MobileNetV2 specifically) and the 2026 fixed-scale sweep (0.40) ([DINO](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.14294); [InfoMin](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.10243); [SSLight](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808); [Two Global Crops](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28187v1)); our optimum near 256 px of 512 (a quarter of the area, half the side) is in that band, so "crop size matters up to about half the frame" is not new in direction. What is new is the separation: no paper varies size and placement independently (Jha and Tuytelaars say so explicitly, [Jha and Tuytelaars](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18228); Two Global Crops does not vary local placement), and the region-placed condition staying 5 points above the best fixed size is the measurement that shows placement is a second factor, not a proxy for size. In remote sensing the only crop-position control is MA3E's +0.15 mAP and +0.19 accuracy over random positions, which its authors call limited ([MA3E](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01946v1)); SatDINO shows narrowing the local scale range costs about 3 points but never moves placement ([SatDINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21402)). For finding 6, the same-dataset detection result, DINOSAR's +1.06 AP on SARDet-100K from content-aware crops and Pu and Xu's +1.3 mAP from same-dataset MAE are both in the range of our +1.2 to +1.6, and our random-window control getting the same gain says that on SARDet-100K detection the benefit is from crop size and domain exposure, which no SAR paper has separated.

## The foundation-model proposer null fits "coarse objectness is enough"

Finding 7 is the cleanest novelty we have. No paper uses a SAR foundation model as a frozen region proposer for another model's SSL views, and no paper in any domain compares a learned or foundation-model proposer against a pixel heuristic and finds them equal. The nearest uses of frozen SAR foundation models are feature extraction for k-NN or LoRA ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)) and SlotSAR, which feeds frozen SARATR-X features into slot attention to separate targets from clutter without using them to pick views or teach a smaller model ([SlotSAR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09298)). Teacher-student work in SAR transfers features or relations from supervised detectors (SURGE: ResNet-18 student 62.9 to 68.0 mAP on SSDD, [SURGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30380); LightKD-SAR at 11.43M parameters and 50.92 mAP on SARDet-100K, [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/32055ce874b248ab8beee9fb3f63cfdd)) or anchors a large ViT to a frozen optical DINOv3 inside SARMAE ([SARMAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16635v2)); "teacher tells the student where to look, student learns on its own" is absent. The closest in spirit is the 2026 A-squared paper, where a small self-supervised ViT's attention proposes crops that a larger ViT embeds, the reverse direction of ours, with the side finding that attention-based localization in DINO-family models gets worse with model size ([A-squared](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03148)).

The null itself is what the literature would predict. Coarse Is Better shows that an almost random grid beats precise ground-truth boxes and nearly matches learned proposals, and that the objectness-scoring backbone "is not strictly necessary" ([Coarse Is Better](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04244)); Addepalli et al. get +3.08 from Grad-CAM saliency crops but note the gain disappears once the reference model's training cost is charged ([Addepalli et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.09866)); masked-image foundation models are weak as fixed feature extractors, which is exactly how SARMAE is used in our proposer ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)). A brightness rule on SAR is a near-ideal "coarse objectness" detector because bright scatterers are the targets, so a frozen foundation model finding more objects but giving no extra accuracy is the expected outcome and a useful one for a compact-model paper: it means the cheap proposer is enough, and the expensive teacher is not needed for this role. Finding 10, the attention maps, has qualitative precedent (SSL models' saliency "spreads over background", [DiLo](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.06638); SAR foundation models lean on background under scene change, [Shaya 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21827)) and is novel as a demonstration that the view policy moves the attention, but a reviewer will not accept pictures alone.

Finally, the compact-model angle is unoccupied ground in all three literatures. The small-model SSL papers (SEED, DisCo, SSLight) study random initialization on ImageNet, where MoCo-v2 on MobileNetV3-Large reaches 36.3 % against 72 % supervised ([SEED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04731v2); [SSLight](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.02808)), and none starts from ImageNet weights and continues SSL in a new domain. The SAR foundation-model papers use ViT-B or larger, with the smallest SSL model on ATRNet-STAR being DINOSAR's 21.4M ViT-S and the smallest on any SAR chip task being the 11M SAR-SimCLR ResNet-18 at 60.3 average nearest-neighbour accuracy, far below the 79.6 of the ViT-B DINOv2 ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)). The only MobileNet SSL paper on SAR uses nine hand-designed pretext tasks on 1,345 SAMPLE chips with no ImageNet baseline ([Sensors 26(1):122](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12787659/)). The one close cousin of our recipe is Mine-JEPA on side-scan sonar: ViT-Tiny and ViT-S started from ImageNet weights, then LeJEPA-style SSL in domain, with the same two qualitative facts we see, that ImageNet initialization before in-domain SSL beats random initialization (0.756 vs 0.725) and that in-domain SSL is not safe by default (DINOv3 drops 0.810 to 0.706 after it) ([Mine-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00383)). It uses ViTs, sonar, and no label sweep, so it is a citation, not a competitor.

## What a reviewer will say is missing

The first request will be a DINOSAR-style baseline. Our proposer must be compared against a gradient-saliency content map with the same window sizes, because otherwise the reviewer cannot tell whether our gain over random placement is the same effect DINOSAR already reported with a different proposer. Running the DINOSAR content map as a third proposer next to the brightness rule and SARMAE attention closes this at low cost, and if it ties the brightness rule, that strengthens the "coarse objectness is enough" story rather than weakening it.

The second request will be a large reference model under our protocol. The paper's premise is compact models, but every published ATRNet-STAR number is from a ViT-B or larger fine-tuned on full labels or at 5 to 10 shots (SOC-50 ladder: SARATR-X 85.2, AFRL-DINOv2 94.9, XuanJian-DINOSAR 96.7, SARATR-X-v2 98.4, [XuanJian-DINOSAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06105); [SARATR-X-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238v4)), so nobody can place our 35 % at 1 % labels on that ladder. Fine-tuning one public checkpoint (SARATR-X or SARMAE, both released) on our exact 1 / 3 / 10 % splits, with CPU latency and parameter counts beside it, is what makes "compact" a claim rather than a constraint. It also forces the disclosure the field now needs: the ATRNet-STAR SOC-40 training chips sit inside the SSL pools of SARATR-X-v2, XuanJian-DINOSAR and AFRL-DINOv2-p3, which alone swings nearest-neighbour accuracy by 18 points ([Inkawhich 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21722)), so our pool exclusions must be stated explicitly and theirs noted.

The third set of requests concerns controls we have partly run. The matched-steps comparison between scene SSL and region SSL (listed as pending in our own notes) is required, because a reviewer will otherwise attribute the gain to more effective updates on cropped views. [Coordinator note, 2026-11-25: this is already covered. Every SSL run on one pool uses the same optimizer step count (146,900 steps on SARDet-100K for DINO, RegionDINO and the controls; 148,500 on the FAIR-CSAR 500-epoch stage), so the comparison is matched in steps; the 100- versus 500-epoch FAIR-CSAR runs cover training length. The paper only needs to say so.] A random-initialization-then-SSL condition is needed if we want to say anything about ImageNet initialization itself, which the SARATR-X ablation and SSL4SAR both show matters ([SARATR-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09365); [SSL4SAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01747)). Seeds should be separated: three label splits do not cover SSL seed variance, and our own notes put hardware noise at about one point, which is the size of the FAIR-CSAR placement effect at 1 and 3 % labels. Significance tests or confidence intervals across splits are expected for differences under 2 points.

The fourth request is an explanation for the ATRNet-STAR versus FAIR-CSAR asymmetry: random windows recover a third of the gain on one and most on the other. The optical literature offers resolution and object density as candidates ([Agrawal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13849); [VHR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08003v1)), and we can test them cheaply by reporting, per pool, how often a random window of the chosen size lands on a bright region; if FAIR-CSAR scenes are dense enough that random windows usually contain a target, the asymmetry becomes a prediction rather than a loose end.

The remaining requests are scope. Does the region recipe help LeJEPA and SparK, or only DINO? The AttMask result that guided selection helps masked objectives more than DINO ([AttMask](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12719)) and our own SparK exception make this a live question. Does the effect survive a larger backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-S), or is it a small-model phenomenon as SSLight would predict? Does it show up on the ATRNet-STAR EOC-Scene split, where every published foundation model is weakest (best 48.3, [XuanJian-DINOSAR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06105)) and where object views should help most? Does the gain vanish at full labels, as the BIDFC snippet suggests for ImageNet fine-tuning above a third of the data ([BIDFC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03627))? And the attention maps need a number, such as the fraction of response mass inside the target box per view policy, with a background-only control in the style of the shortcut study ([Shaya 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21827)).

## Conclusion

The novelty check changes the paper's centre of gravity. "Content-aware views help SAR SSL" is now DINOSAR's sentence, and a paper whose headline is region-placed DINO on SAR scenes will be read as a smaller-scale replication with a different proposer. The sentence nobody else can write is the diagnostic one: a strong SSL recipe on a large SAR scene archive makes a small ImageNet-initialised network worse on another dataset's object chips, the damage is specific to scene-centric pools and absent on chip pools, it is undone by cutting the views around bright regions and discarding the whole-scene views, the fix is mostly about where and how big the views are rather than which model proposes them, and the same recipe gives nothing extra on same-dataset detection. That is a paper about pool composition and view policy for compact models, with DINOSAR cited as the large-scale confirmation that content-aware views help, not as a competitor.

The gap between the current state and a main-track submission is three experiments, not a new idea: a gradient-saliency proposer baseline, one public SAR foundation model fine-tuned under our 1 / 3 / 10 % protocol with cost numbers, and the matched-steps run. The FAIR-CSAR asymmetry and the SparK exception are the two results that currently look like loose ends and could become the most interesting part of the discussion if we measure object density per pool and separate masked from joint-embedding objectives.

## TL;DR

**What we checked.** Whether anyone has already published the ten things we measured with a small MobileNetV2 trained on SAR images: that self-supervised learning on the right unlabeled images helps a lot with few labels, that learning on big scene images can make things worse than plain ImageNet weights, that cutting the learning views around bright spots in the scene fixes that, and that a big SAR foundation model is no better than a simple brightness rule at choosing those spots. Five researchers scanned about 80 papers from 2020 to November 2026, including everything citing ATRNet-STAR, SARDet-100K and FAIR-CSAR.

**What we found.** Nobody has done the scene-to-chip comparison, the random-placement control with matched window sizes, the window-size sweep separated from placement, the foundation-model-versus-brightness-rule test, or anything at MobileNetV2 size with 1 / 3 / 10 % labels. One paper, DINOSAR (November 2026), did get there first on the core idea: it picks DINO views by image content instead of at random, on 7 million SAR tiles with a ViT, and shows it beats random crops on ATRNet-STAR and SARDet-100K. SAMBA uses nearly the same brightness threshold we use, but to decide what to mask in a masked autoencoder on chips, not where to cut views on scenes. In natural images, "crops that roughly contain objects beat random crops, random crops of the right size get part of the gain, and exact boxes are not better than rough ones" is well known, and our numbers agree with it.

**How good or bad this is for the story.** Mixed but workable. We lost the right to say "we are the first to choose SAR SSL views by content". We keep the stronger and more specific claim: for a small network, SSL on a scene archive hurts transfer to object chips unless the views are cut around objects and the whole-scene views are dropped, size and placement are separate knobs, a cheap brightness rule is as good as a frozen foundation model for the placement, and none of this matters on chip pools or for same-dataset detection. No published paper contradicts any of our ten findings; several predict them.

**What to do next.** Three runs before writing: add a DINOSAR-style gradient-saliency proposer as a third option next to the brightness rule and SARMAE; fine-tune one public SAR foundation model (SARATR-X or SARMAE) on our exact 1 / 3 / 10 % splits and report its accuracy, parameters and CPU latency next to MobileNetV2; finish the matched-steps comparison. Then measure how often a random window hits a bright region in each pool to explain why random placement nearly works on FAIR-CSAR but not on ATRNet-STAR, put a number on the attention maps, and state in the paper that ATRNet-STAR training chips were excluded from our SSL pools while they sit inside the pools of the published foundation models. With those in hand, write the paper around pool composition and view policy for compact models, and cite DINOSAR as confirmation at scale rather than as a rival.
